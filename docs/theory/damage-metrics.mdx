Nucleide computes activation inventories and gamma sources; the matching first-wall
question — how many displacements, and how much gas, a neutron spectrum produces in a
material — is answered by the `nucleide.damage` kernel. This page pins the equation
set: the Lindhard damage-energy partition, the NRT-dpa standard of Norgett, Robinson &
Torrens<Citation id="nrt-1975" />, the arc-dpa efficiency correction of Nordlund et
al.<Citation id="nordlund-2018" />, and the spectral-folding conventions that turn
caller-condensed cross sections into displacements per atom (dpa), atomic parts per
million (appm) of helium or hydrogen, and He/dpa ratios — plus the uncertainty
propagation hooked over the folds. Every material constant is caller data; nothing is
fitted or vendored here.

## Overview

A neutron spectrum folded with a material's damage response cross sections gives the
displacement dose in dpa; folded with gas-production cross sections it gives the
helium/hydrogen inventory in appm; the ratio of the two tracks embrittlement-relevant
helium per unit damage. The crate has two layers of pure functions over caller
inputs:

- closed-form primary-damage physics — the Lindhard partition (Robinson fit), the
  NRT displacement function, and the arc-dpa correction;
- spectral folds — `nrt_dpa` / `arc_dpa` / `gas_appm` / `he_dpa_ratio` over
  `(flux, response, bounds)` slices with piecewise-constant-per-group semantics.

A third module, `fold_uq`, propagates caller-block uncertainty through the folds by
reusing the shared seeded-sampler machinery documented on the UQ-lite page — no new
sampling code.

## Mathematical details

### The Lindhard partition and damage energy

A recoiling lattice atom loses part of its energy to electronic excitation before it
can displace further atoms. The Lindhard partition divides the primary knock-on atom
(PKA) energy `T` between that ionizing part and the displacement (damage) energy
`T_dam`<Citation id="lindhard-1963" />; Nucleide uses Robinson's analytic fit to the
partition, with constants as documented by Griffin (SAND2016-2269 §3.2)<Citation
id="griffin-sand2016" />:

$$
P(\varepsilon) = \frac{1}{1 + k_L\, g(\varepsilon)},
\qquad
T_{dam} = T\, P(\varepsilon)
\tag{P1}
$$

$$
k_L = 0.0793\,
\frac{Z_R^{2/3}\, Z_L^{1/2}\, (A_R + A_L)^{3/2}}
     {(Z_R^{2/3} + Z_L^{2/3})^{3/4}\, A_R^{3/2}\, A_L^{1/2}}
\tag{P2}
$$

$$
E_L = 30.724\ \mathrm{eV} \cdot
Z_R Z_L\, (Z_R^{2/3} + Z_L^{2/3})^{1/2}\, \frac{A_R + A_L}{A_L},
\qquad
\varepsilon = T / E_L
\tag{P3}
$$

$$
g(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon + 3.4008\, \varepsilon^{1/6}
+ 0.40244\, \varepsilon^{3/4}
\tag{P4}
$$

with `Z_R, A_R` the recoil nuclide and `Z_L, A_L` the lattice nuclide (damage physics
sees only `Z` and `A` — the metastable state index of a nuclide key is ignored).

- `P → 1` as `T → 0` (only logarithmically — the `ε^{1/6}` term still costs ~0.7% at
  1 µeV for iron) and `P → 0` as `T → ∞`; the damage energy saturates at `E_L/k_L`
  and never exceeds `T`.
- The constants follow Griffin §3.2, which also documents the NJOY manual's `k_L`
  exponent typo: the combined mass term in the numerator of (P2) carries exponent
  3/2, not 2/3. Nucleide uses the corrected 3/2.
- Energies are electron-volts throughout the damage functions; the spectral folds use
  MeV group bounds because neutron group structures are tabulated in MeV.
- Damage energy follows the SPECTER/NJOY lower-integration-bound-zero convention
  (Griffin §2.5)<Citation id="griffin-sand2016" />: recoil energy below `E_d` still
  counts toward damage energy — it just does not displace.

### The NRT displacement function

The Norgett–Robinson–Torrens standard<Citation id="nrt-1975" /> modifies the
Kinchin–Pease count with a displacement efficiency `κ = 0.8` and the Lindhard damage
energy:

$$
N_d(T) =
\begin{cases}
0 & T < E_d \\
1 & E_d \le T < 2E_d/\kappa \\
\kappa\, T_{dam}(T) / (2E_d) & T \ge 2E_d/\kappa
\end{cases}
\qquad \kappa = 0.8
\tag{N1}
$$

Nucleide evaluates (N1) for a self-recoil target, or for an arbitrary recoil/lattice
pair (a transmutation recoil stopped in a host lattice). The piecewise boundaries sit
on the PKA energy `T` — at `E_d` and `2E_d/κ` — while the high branch evaluates
`κ·T_dam/(2E_d)` on the damage energy. That boundary convention follows SPECTER/NJOY
(Griffin §2.5)<Citation id="griffin-sand2016" />, not the arc-dpa paper verbatim
(its Eqs. (1) and (5) write both boundaries on the damage energy `T_d`). Two
consequences are pinned as known artifacts of the convention, not bugs: the `N_d = 1`
plateau covers PKA energies whose damage energy is still below `E_d` (a
damage-energy boundary would return 0 there), and the small downward step at the
junction `T = 2E_d/κ` is the known NRT construction.

### The arc-dpa correction

Molecular-dynamics (MD) cascades show the NRT count overestimates surviving defects
because recombination inside the cascade is ignored. Nordlund et al. (2018, CC BY
4.0) repair this with an efficiency function fitted to MD data — Nucleide implements
Eqs. (5)–(7) of that paper<Citation id="nordlund-2018" />:

$$
\xi(T_d) = (1 - c_{arc}) \left( \frac{T_d}{2E_d/\kappa} \right)^{b_{arc}} + c_{arc}
\tag{A1}
$$

By construction `ξ(2E_d/κ) = 1` (junction with the plateau), `ξ → c_arc` as
`T_d → ∞` (the MD saturation value), and `b_arc < 0` makes `ξ` monotone decreasing.
`b_arc` and `c_arc` are material constants the caller supplies from MD or experiment;
the constructor enforces the fitted domain — `b_arc` finite and negative, `c_arc`
finite in `(0, 1)` — and the crate ships no fitted table. The corrected count is the
NRT piecewise form with the high branch multiplied by `ξ(T_dam(T))`:

$$
N_{d,arc}(T) =
\begin{cases}
0 & T < E_d \\
1 & E_d \le T < 2E_d/\kappa \\
\kappa\, T_{dam}(T)\, \xi(T_{dam}(T)) / (2E_d) & T \ge 2E_d/\kappa
\end{cases}
\tag{A2}
$$

One artifact of the PKA-energy boundary convention carries over: at `T = 2E_d/κ` the
efficiency overshoots 1 because `T_dam < T` there (the paper's damage-energy form
stays at or below 1). `ξ(0)` diverges — zero to a negative power — and raises a clear
error, never `inf`.

### Spectral folds

The closed forms above are what a nuclear-data pipeline would tabulate into per-group
response cross sections; the crate itself folds a caller-supplied multigroup flux with
caller-supplied responses under fixed conventions:

- `bounds` holds `G + 1` group boundaries in MeV, strictly increasing. Group `g`
  spans `[bounds[g], bounds[g+1])`. Bounds are validated and document the grid; the
  fold is piecewise-constant per group, so a group's width never enters the sum.
- `flux[g]` is the per-group integrated flux (n/cm²/s through group `g`), i.e.
  `∫_group φ(E) dE` — not a per-lethargy or per-unit-energy density.
- `response[g]` is the group response cross section in barns, condensed by the caller
  consistently with the flux spectrum — a SPECTER/NJOY-class pipeline for
  displacement cross sections, an activation library for gas production. The kernel
  multiplies; it never re-weights within a group.

$$
m = s \sum_g \phi_g\, r_g,
\qquad
s =
\begin{cases}
10^{-24}\, t_{exp} & \text{dpa} \\
10^{-18}\, t_{exp} & \text{gas appm}
\end{cases}
\tag{F1}
$$

`10⁻²⁴` converts barns to cm² (per target atom); the gas scale adds the
per-atom → parts-per-million factor `10⁶`. `t_exp` is the exposure time at the
tabulated flux; pass `1.0` with a fluence parked in `flux` for a fluence fold.
`nrt_dpa` and `arc_dpa` share (F1) — they differ only in which displacement function
built the response. `gas_appm` counts whichever gas (He or H) the caller's response
counts, one nuclide's production per target atom. `he_dpa_ratio` folds the He and
damage responses over the same flux and divides; a damage fold of exactly zero raises
a clear error, never `inf`.

### Uncertainty propagation

`fold_uq` perturbs the stacked `[flux, response]` vector (dimension `2G`) with seeded
draws from a multivariate normal (MVN) distribution — the shared `nucleide-linalg`
sampler the UQ-lite page documents — using relative-unit perturbations applied as
`nominal·(1 + δ)` (the SANDY convention), then refolds each draw. For the three
linear folds the expectation under the Gaussian block is exact, and the standard
deviation is available to first order:

$$
E[m] = m_0 + s \sum_g f_g r_g
\left( \mu_{f,g} + \mu_{r,g} + \mu_{f,g}\, \mu_{r,g} + C_{g,G+g} \right)
\tag{U1}
$$

$$
\mathrm{std}[m] = s\,\sqrt{J C J^{T}},
\qquad
J = \left[\, (f \odot r) \odot (1 + \mu_r) \;\middle|\; (f \odot r) \odot (1 + \mu_f) \,\right]
\tag{U2}
$$

with `μ` the block mean, `C` the `(2G)×(2G)` covariance, and `⊙` the elementwise
product. For small relative blocks the quartic term neglected by (U2) sits orders of
magnitude inside the check width (in-crate tests size it at ~3×10⁻⁴ of the linear
term). The returned summary carries the nominal value, the sample mean and standard
deviation over the draws, the exact expectation (U1), the first-order propagation
(U2), and honest `k`-standard-error checks: the sample mean against the expectation
within `k·std/√n`, and the sample standard deviation against (U2) within
`k·std/√(2(n−1))`. A draw that leaves the physical domain (negative flux or response)
raises the fold's named error, never a silent clip — callers keep covariance blocks
in the small-perturbation regime. The He/dpa ratio is a nonlinear statistic: its UQ
is not yet supported and raises a clear named error.

## Algorithm

The physics layer is pure functions: validate (finite non-negative `T`, `E_d > 0`,
valid nuclide keys, arc constants in the fitted domain), then evaluate (P1)–(A2) —
the partition at the PKA energy, the piecewise branches against PKA-energy
thresholds. The fold layer validates shape, finiteness, sign, and monotonicity first
— empty input, mismatched lengths, non-finite values, negative flux or response,
non-increasing bounds, and non-positive exposure each raise a named error — then
accumulates `Σ f·r` in one pass with the metric scale. There is no division anywhere
in the fold, so a zero-flux group contributes exactly `0.0` and cannot produce
`NaN`/`inf`; the ratio computes two folds and checks the denominator. The UQ layer
draws once (Cholesky factorization primary, eigenvalue-clip fallback), perturbs and
refolds per draw, and compares unbiased sample moments against (U1)–(U2).

Correctness checks: in-crate tests and the `damage_vs_specter.py` harness replay the
synthetic checks labeled G1–G5 — G1 the partition and NRT branches against an
independent in-script transcription of the published Robinson-fit constants
(tolerance 10⁻¹²), G2 the arc construction (junction value exactly 1, saturation at
`c_arc`, monotone decrease, high branch = NRT × ξ), G3 the folds against hand sums,
G4 the invariants (zero-flux groups exact; the zero-dpa ratio and malformed inputs
raising named errors), and G5 the pinned-seed UQ checks including bit-identical
reruns. The external SPECTER cross-check is described under Related work.

## Worked example: Fe-56 anchors and a three-group fold

All inputs below are synthetic: an Fe-56 self-recoil with an assumed average
threshold displacement energy `E_d = 40 eV`, assumed arc constants `b_arc = −0.55`,
`c_arc = 0.3`, and a hand-written three-group flux — the crate ships no material data.

```python
import nucleide.damage as dmg

ED = 40.0  # eV, assumed average threshold displacement energy
B_ARC, C_ARC = -0.55, 0.3  # assumed MD-style arc constants (fitted domain: b < 0, 0 < c < 1)

# (N1), (A1), (A2): piecewise displacement functions, Fe-56 self-recoil
print(dmg.nrt_displacements(20.0, ED, "Fe56"))  # 0.0  (T < E_d)
print(dmg.nrt_displacements(40.0, ED, "Fe56"))  # 1.0  (T = E_d)
print(dmg.nrt_displacements(100.0 * (1 - 1e-12), ED, "Fe56"))  # 1.0 (2*E_d/0.8 = 100 eV)
t_dam = dmg.damage_energy(1.0e5, "Fe56", "Fe56")
print(t_dam)  # 61865.089085593674 eV
print(dmg.nrt_displacements(1.0e5, ED, "Fe56"))  # 618.6508908559368
print(dmg.arc_efficiency(100.0, ED, B_ARC, C_ARC))  # 1.0 exactly (A1 junction)
print(dmg.arc_displacements(1.0e5, ED, "Fe56", B_ARC, C_ARC))  # 198.22076705250228

# (F1): three-group fold; hand sum 1e12*100 + 2e12*200 + 4e12*50 = 7e14
bounds = [0.0, 0.1, 1.0, 20.0]
flux = [1.0e12, 2.0e12, 4.0e12]
resp = [100.0, 200.0, 50.0]
print(dmg.nrt_dpa(flux, resp, bounds, 2.0))  # 2*1e-24*7e14 = 1.4e-9
print(dmg.gas_appm(flux, resp, bounds, 2.0))  # 2*1e-18*7e14 = 1.4e-3
print(dmg.he_dpa_ratio(flux, resp, resp, bounds, 2.0))  # 1.4e-3 / 1.4e-9 = 1.0e6
print(dmg.nrt_dpa(flux, resp, [0.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0], 2.0))  # 1.4e-9 (bounds never enter)
dead = [0.0, 2.0e12, 0.0]
print(dmg.nrt_dpa(dead, resp, bounds, 1.0))  # 3.9999999999999996e-10
print(dmg.nrt_dpa(dead, resp, bounds, 1.0) == 1e-24 * (2e12 * 200.0))  # True

# (U1), (U2): seeded MVN over the stacked [flux, response] block, 1% std per group
mean = [0.0] * 6
cov = [[1e-4 if i == j else 0.0 for j in range(6)] for i in range(6)]
out = dmg.fold_uq("nrt_dpa", flux, resp, bounds, 2.0, mean, cov, n=4000, seed=20260915, k=5.0)
print(out["expected"], out["analytic_std"], out["passed"])
```

Hand verification:

- The anchors follow from (N1) alone: `2E_d/κ = 2×40/0.8 = 100 eV`, so 20 eV is below
  threshold, 40 eV displaces exactly once, and the plateau holds right up to the
  junction.
- At `T = 10⁵ eV` the partition gives `T_dam = 61865.089 eV` (≈ 0.62 of the PKA
  energy survives electronic losses), and the high branch is
  `0.8 × 61865.089 / (2×40) = 618.6508909` — here `κ/(2E_d) = 1/(100 eV)`, so the
  value is just `T_dam` in units of 100 eV. An independent transcription of the
  published constants — `E_L = 30.724·26²·(2·26^{2/3})^{1/2}·2 = 174031.25 eV`,
  `k_L = 0.0793·2^{3/4}·26^{2/3}/56^{1/2} = 0.156411` — reproduces the partition
  `P(10 keV) = 1/(1 + k_L·g(0.057461)) = 0.7424990` to all printed digits.
- For the arc count, `ξ(T_dam) = 0.7×(618.65089)^{−0.55} + 0.3 = 0.3204081` and
  `618.6508909 × 0.3204081 = 198.2208`; at the junction `ξ(100 eV) = 1` exactly.
- The fold hand sum is `7×10¹⁴` barns·n/cm²/s, giving
  `dpa = 2×10⁻²⁴×7×10¹⁴ = 1.4×10⁻⁹` and `appm = 2×10⁻¹⁸×7×10¹⁴ = 1.4×10⁻³`; the
  ratio is the per-atom→ppm factor, `1.0×10⁶ appm/dpa` to floating rounding. Regridding the bounds
  changes nothing — the bounds document the grid and never enter the sum. With the
  first and third groups dead, the fold equals the literal hand product
  `1e-24 * (2e12 * 200.0)` exactly (`True` on the equality check).
- For the UQ block, (U1) with zero means gives `E[m] = m₀ = 1.4×10⁻⁹` exactly, and
  (U2) gives `std = 2×10⁻²⁴ × √((10¹⁴)² + (4×10¹⁴)² + (2×10¹⁴)²) × √(2×10⁻⁴) =
  1.2961×10⁻¹¹` — matching the reported `analytic_std` to all printed digits, with
  both `k`-standard-error checks passing.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **PKA-energy boundary convention**: the piecewise boundaries sit on the PKA energy
  (`E_d`, `2E_d/κ`) with the high branch evaluated on the damage energy — the
  SPECTER/NJOY convention, not the arc-dpa paper's damage-energy boundaries. Pinned
  artifacts: the `N_d = 1` plateau covers PKA energies whose damage energy is below
  `E_d`, and the arc efficiency slightly exceeds 1 just above the junction (because
  `T_dam < T` there). The small downward step at `T = 2E_d/κ` is the known NRT
  construction.
- **Units**: the damage functions take electron-volts; group bounds are MeV.
- **Caller-supplied constants**: `E_d`, `b_arc`, `c_arc`, and every response table
  are caller data; arc constants are validated against the MD-fitted domain
  (`b_arc < 0`, `0 < c_arc < 1`) and no fitted parameter table ships. The partition
  needs only `Z` and `A` of recoil and lattice.
- **Piecewise-constant folds**: the kernel never re-weights within a group, and
  `bounds` document the grid without entering the sum; responses must be condensed
  consistently with the flux spectrum by the caller's own pipeline.
- **Zero and sign handling**: zero-flux groups contribute exactly `0.0`; the He/dpa
  ratio at zero damage raises a clear error — never infinity; the arc efficiency at
  zero damage energy diverges by construction and raises a clear error — never
  infinity; negative flux or response raises a clear error, never clamped.
- **Uncertainty scope**: UQ covers the three linear folds. The He/dpa ratio is a
  nonlinear statistic and its UQ is not yet supported (a clear named error). MVN
  draws that leave the physical domain surface as the fold's named error, never a
  silent clip — keep covariance blocks small (the first-order (U2) neglects a
  quartic term ~3×10⁻⁴ of the linear term at the in-crate sizing).
- **Out of scope**: PKA-spectrum solving, displacement-table vendoring, transport
  solving, and evaluated-data consumption (TENDL/JEFF/EAF/IRDFF) — callers bring
  their own response tables. ASTM E693/E521 are paywalled standards referenced by
  designation string only.
- **Thin facades**: `nucleide.damage` (Python) exposes the crate one-to-one; no
  physics beyond the folds above.

## Related work beyond Nucleide

**SPECTER** (Greenwood & Smither, ANL/FPP/TM-197, 1985 — a US-government work, public
domain)<Citation id="specter-1985" /> is the fusion-community damage calculator whose
printed HFIR-CTR32 results (Tables V/VI, total fluence 4.78373×10²² n/cm²) serve as
the external correctness check for the folds in the repo validation harness: a
handful of transcribed spectrum-averaged dpa and gas-production cross sections are
folded one-group at report print precision (five significant digits, 10⁻⁴ relative
tolerance) and matched against the report's printed dpa, GAS(APPM), and Fe He/dpa
values. SPECTER is a correctness-check source and the source of one opt-in
fallback table — the Table VII spectrum-averaged damage-energy cross
sections for 24 elements (plus the Table II threshold energies), exposed
through `nucleide.damage` for callers with no damage-data pipeline of their
own and never consulted implicitly — and when the report text is
unavailable the check is recorded as skipped, never a silent pass. ASTM
E693/E521 are the paywalled displacement-dose standards of record,
referenced by designation only.

Adjacent capabilities Nucleide does not implement: PKA-spectrum solving (the
GPL-licensed fispact-org PKA evaluator was never read), transport solving,
and per-group displacement tables built from an evaluated library — that
NJOY/SPECTER-class pipeline remains the caller's job.
