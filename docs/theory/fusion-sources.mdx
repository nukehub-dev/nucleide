Transport codes need to know where neutrons are born and with what energy. This page
pins the physics behind `nucleide.plasma_source`, the tokamak fusion-neutron source
capability: temperature-broadened D-D (2.45 MeV) and D-T (14.1 MeV) spectra, a
parametric Miller-geometry plasma driven by caller-supplied L/H/A-mode profiles,
seeded sampling to particle vectors, and emission of MCNP source-definition cards
(`SDEF`) and Serpent source cards (`src`) with an emission report that quantifies
what the cards preserve and what they lose.

The spectrum of thermonuclear neutrons is Gaussian to good approximation, with a
width proportional to $\sqrt{T_i}$<Citation id="brysk-1973" />. Nucleide implements
the closed-form coefficient fits of Ballabio et al.<Citation id="ballabio-1998" />,
which refine the Brysk scaling with a small mean-energy shift and a weakly
temperature-dependent width; at $T_i = 0$ the spectrum collapses to the
monoenergetic nominal line. The spatial side is the public ITER/EU-DEMO
parametrization of Fausser et al. — a Miller flux-surface map with L/H/A-mode
density and temperature profiles<Citation id="fausser-2012" /> — and the neutron
emission is reactivity-weighted with the Bosch–Hale fits in the
Atzeni–Meyer-ter-Vehn parametrization<Citation id="bosch-hale-1992" />. All
equations and coefficients are transcribed as published facts and pinned by
independently recomputed reference values; no simulator source was read to
produce them.

## Overview

`nucleide.plasma_source` builds fusion neutron sources at three levels of fidelity.
The simple tier is a point source or an axisymmetric ring on the full torus (a
tokamak midplane circle): positions are exact, and the only physics is the
energy spectrum. The parametric tier models the plasma cross-section with Miller
flux surfaces and caller-supplied L/H/A-mode profiles of ion density and
temperature; birth positions weight the local neutron production, so the source
follows the profiles and the geometry rather than a hand-drawn shape. The
lattice tier drops axisymmetry itself for non-axisymmetric (stellarator-class)
plasmas: the caller supplies the full 3D birth distribution as an explicit
point cloud, and the same fuel mixtures, spectra, and card emission apply per
node. All tiers
sample seeded, deterministic particle vectors (position, direction, energy,
weight) and emit `SDEF` and `src` cards with an emission report.

Units follow the transport-code card convention: lengths in centimetres, energies
in MeV, ion temperature in keV. Profile densities are caller data in m⁻³ (the
Fausser convention) and converted internally; reactivity is reported in m³/s.

## Mathematical details

### S1 — Temperature-broadened neutron spectrum

The spectrum of thermonuclear neutrons is Gaussian to good approximation, with a
width proportional to $\sqrt{T_i}$<Citation id="brysk-1973" />. Nucleide uses the
closed-form fits of Ballabio et al., Table III<Citation id="ballabio-1998" />,
which refine the Brysk scaling with a small mean-energy shift and a weakly
temperature-dependent width:

$$
\bar{E}(T_i) = E_0 + \frac{a_1\,T_i^{2/3}}{1 + a_2\,T_i^{a_3}} + a_4\,T_i,
\tag{S1a}
$$

$$
\mathrm{FWHM}(T_i) = w_0\left(1 + \frac{b_1\,T_i^{2/3}}{1 + b_2\,T_i^{b_3}} + b_4\,T_i\right)\sqrt{T_i},
\qquad
\sigma = \frac{\mathrm{FWHM}}{2\sqrt{2\ln 2}}.
\tag{S1b}
$$

Here $T_i$ is in keV and the mean shift is computed in keV and added to $E_0$ in
MeV; $(a_1, \dots, a_4)$ and $(w_0, b_1, \dots, b_4)$ are the published Table III
coefficients for each reaction. $E_0$ is the nominal cold line: 14.021 MeV for
D-T and 2.4495 MeV for D-D — the "14.1 MeV" and "2.45 MeV" lines of the
literature. At $T_i = 0$ both corrections vanish and the spectrum is the
monoenergetic nominal line.

### S2 — Thermonuclear reactivity

Emission weights the profiles by the thermonuclear reactivity
$\langle\sigma v\rangle$ of Bosch &amp; Hale<Citation id="bosch-hale-1992" />,
evaluated in the compact parametrization reproduced by Atzeni &amp;
Meyer-ter-Vehn:

$$
\langle\sigma v\rangle(T) = C_1\,\eta^{-5/6}\,\xi^2\,e^{-3\,\eta^{1/3}\xi}\ \ [\mathrm{m^3/s}],
\qquad
\xi = A\,T^{-1/3},
\qquad
\eta(T) = 1 - \frac{\mathrm{num}(T)}{\mathrm{den}(T)}.
\tag{S2}
$$

$T$ is in keV and num, den are per-reaction rational correction polynomials:

| Reaction | $C_1$ [m³/s] | $A$ | num($T$) | den($T$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D-T | 643.41e-22 | 6.6610 | $-1.0675\mathrm{e}{-4}T^3 + 4.6064\mathrm{e}{-3}T^2 + 1.5136\mathrm{e}{-2}T$ | $1.366\mathrm{e}{-5}T^3 + 1.35\mathrm{e}{-2}T^2 + 7.5189\mathrm{e}{-2}T + 1$ |
| D-D | 3.5741e-22 | 6.2696 | $5.8577\mathrm{e}{-3}T$ | $-2.964\mathrm{e}{-6}T^2 + 7.6822\mathrm{e}{-3}T + 1$ |

The reactivity vanishes as $T \to 0$ (the exponential beats the power-law
growth), which the parametric sampler relies on at cold separatrix temperatures;
the crate returns exactly 0 at $T = 0$. The D-D row is the published D-D fit —
the neutron-relevant D(d,n)³He branch shares it to the precision of the
coefficients. Where the fit leaves its validity domain (the D-D $\eta$ goes
non-positive on roughly 965–2720 keV, far above the published range) the crate
raises a clear out-of-domain error instead of returning NaN.

### S3 — Miller flux-surface map and volume element

The parametric plasma cross-section is the Miller map of Fausser et
al.<Citation id="fausser-2012" />, in the spelling common to that paper and the
MIT `openmc-plasma-source` package:

$$
\Delta(a) = \mathrm{esh}\left(1 - \frac{a^2}{a_\text{minor}^2}\right),
\qquad
R(a,\theta) = R_0 + a\cos(\theta + \delta\sin\theta) + \Delta(a),
\qquad
Z(a,\theta) = \kappa\,a\sin\theta.
\tag{S3}
$$

Major radius $R_0$, minor-radius label $a \in [0, a_\text{minor}]$, elongation
$\kappa$, triangularity $\delta$, and Shafranov factor $\mathrm{esh}$ are the
shape parameters (lengths in cm). Neutron production lives on flux surfaces, so
the volume element matters:

$$
\frac{dV}{da\,d\theta\,d\varphi} = R\,|J|,
\qquad
J = \frac{\partial R}{\partial a}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\theta}
  - \frac{\partial R}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial a}.
\tag{S3-V}
$$

— the poloidal Jacobian times the toroidal radius $R$. The $R$ factor is
mandatory: without it the core is over-represented. Closed forms pinned by the
crate: in the torus limit ($\kappa = 1$, $\delta = 0$, $\mathrm{esh} = 0$)
$J = a$ exactly; at $\delta = 0$ (any $\kappa$, $\mathrm{esh}$)

$$
J = \kappa\,a\left(1 - \frac{2\,\mathrm{esh}\,a\cos\theta}{a_\text{minor}^2}\right),
$$

the cross-section area is $\pi\kappa a^2$, and the torus volume is
$2\pi^2\kappa a^2 R_0$ for any $\mathrm{esh}$ — the Shafranov terms integrate
out, so the shift moves surfaces outboard without changing the volume. (The
$|J| = J$ step needs $J > 0$ everywhere, which the crate's
$|\mathrm{esh}| < a_\text{minor}/2$ validation guarantees.) The map
is up-down symmetric and the vertical extent is $\max|Z| = \kappa\,a_\text{minor}$.

### S4 — L/H/A-mode plasma profiles

Profiles over the minor radius are the L/H/A-mode parametrizations of Fausser
et al.<Citation id="fausser-2012" />. L mode is a single power-law falloff:

$$
f(r) = f_c\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{a^2}\right)^{\alpha},
\tag{S4-L}
$$

H and A mode (same algebra; the modes differ by parameter choice, not formula)
add a pedestal at $r_\text{ped}$ and a linear edge:

$$
f(r) = \begin{cases}
f_\text{ped} + (f_c - f_\text{ped})\left(1 - (r/r_\text{ped})^2\right)^{\alpha} & r \le r_\text{ped}, \\[6pt]
f_\text{sep} + (f_\text{ped} - f_\text{sep})\,\dfrac{a - r}{a - r_\text{ped}} & r > r_\text{ped},
\end{cases}
\tag{S4-H}
$$

the temperature core instead carries a $\beta_T$ exponent,
$(1 - (r/r_\text{ped})^{\beta_T})^{\alpha_T}$. Profiles are *inputs*: the caller
supplies the centre, peaking, pedestal, and separatrix values and the mode
label; nothing here computes or fits profiles, and no equilibrium is solved.
Density is caller data in m⁻³ and temperature in keV.

### S5 — Reactivity-weighted emission strength

Neutron production at minor radius $r$ follows the local ion density and
temperature through the reaction rate:

$$
S(r) = f_\text{fuel}\, n_i(r)^2\, \langle\sigma v\rangle\bigl(T_i(r)\bigr).
\tag{S5}
$$

$f_\text{fuel} = 1/4$ for equimolar D-T ($n_D = n_T = n_i/2$) and $1/2$ for pure
D-D — the ½ avoids double-counting identical reactant pairs and follows the
Fausser/`openmc-plasma-source` strength convention. A D/T fuel mixture with
fractions $f_D + f_T = 1$ instead weights both branches of the rate:

$$
S(r) = n_i(r)^2 \left[ f_D f_T\, \langle\sigma v\rangle_{DT}\bigl(T_i(r)\bigr)
      + \frac{f_D^2}{2}\, \langle\sigma v\rangle_{DD}\bigl(T_i(r)\bigr) \right],
\tag{S5-M}
$$

the standard $n_a n_b \langle\sigma v\rangle$ rate with the $1/(1+\delta_{ab})$
same-species guard (the DRESS rate construction of Eriksson et al., and the
form upstream `openmc-plasma-source` implements). At $f_D = f_T = 1/2$ the first
term reproduces the equimolar factor $1/4$ exactly, and at $f_D = 1$ the second
term reproduces the pure D-D factor $1/2$; birth energies draw from the local
mixture of the two Ballabio lines, branch by branch. Birth positions weight
$S \cdot R \cdot |J|$, the volume element of (S3-V); the total relative
strength is its toroidal integral,
$S_\text{tot} = 2\pi \int_0^a\!\int_0^{2\pi} S\,R\,|J|\,d\theta\,dr$ — arbitrary
global scale, but ratios between configurations are meaningful. Because
$\langle\sigma v\rangle \to 0$ as $T_i \to 0$, a cold separatrix makes no
neutrons.

### S5-T — Deuterium hot-tail fraction

Auxiliary-heated plasmas carry a non-thermal tail that a pure Maxwellian
mis-models. The one pinned non-Maxwellian shape<Citation id="eriksson-2016" />
splits the deuterium population into a bulk $(1 - \eta)$ at $T_D$ and a hot
tail $\eta$ at $T_\mathrm{tail}$ (uniform scalars, flat in minor radius),
while tritium stays Maxwellian at $T_T$. Each sub-pair reacts at its own
mass-weighted relative temperature:

$$
T_{DT} = T_D + \tfrac{2}{5}(T_T - T_D),
\qquad
T_{DTt} = T_\mathrm{tail} + \tfrac{2}{5}(T_T - T_\mathrm{tail}),
\qquad
T_\mathrm{mix} = \tfrac{1}{2}(T_D + T_\mathrm{tail}),
\tag{S5-T}
$$

and the strength rule expands into the bulk/tail sub-rates:

$$
S(r) = n_i(r)^2 \left[ f_D f_T\, \bigl((1-\eta)\langle\sigma v\rangle_{DT}(T_{DT}) + \eta\,\langle\sigma v\rangle_{DT}(T_{DTt})\bigr) + \tfrac{f_D^2}{2}\, \bigl((1-\eta)^2\langle\sigma v\rangle_{DD}(T_D) + 2\eta(1-\eta)\langle\sigma v\rangle_{DD}(T_\mathrm{mix}) + \eta^2\langle\sigma v\rangle_{DD}(T_\mathrm{tail})\bigr) \right].
$$

The D-T arm carries two spectrum lines (at $T_{DT}$ and $T_{DTt}$) and the
D-D arm three (at $T_D$, $T_\mathrm{mix}$, $T_\mathrm{tail}$), weighted by the
same sub-rate rule, so strengths, sampled energies, card marginals, and the
axis summary stay consistent. Each line is the Ballabio Gaussian at its
effective temperature — an approximation in the same spirit as the
species-pair lines, since the published fits assumed single-temperature
Maxwellians. The pinned hand vector (flat profile, $n = 10^{20}$ m⁻³,
$f_D = 0.7$, $f_T = 0.3$, $T_D = 20$ keV, $T_T = 30$ keV, $\eta = 0.05$,
$T_\mathrm{tail} = 60$ keV) gates the rate at $10^{-12}$ relative, and
$\eta = 0$ reproduces the no-tail mixture kernel exactly.

### S6 — Arbitrary-3D birth-rate lattice

The lattice tier drops the last tokamak assumption, axisymmetry. The caller
supplies the full 3D birth distribution as a point list — positions,
relative birth-rate weights, and per-point ion temperatures — and the same
fuel mixtures, Ballabio lines, and card emission apply per node. An
optional field-period symmetry declares the points to be one base-sector
cloud replicated $n$ times around the machine axis (toroidal angles
identified modulo $2\pi/n$), with the symmetric total scaled by $n$; $n = 1$
is the identity. Anchors pinned by tests: a two-point hand vector (rates
2 and 1, total 3, mean birth position $(100, 0, 25)$, mono 14.021 MeV line),
single-point reproduction of the point source, ring-cloud convergence to
the analytic ring moments, and bit-for-bit reproduction of the expanded
full-cloud stream by the folded base-sector stream. Cards carry the cloud's
cylindrical-$R$/vertical/energy marginals with a lattice row in the
emission report, exactly like the parametric product-form cards.

### S7 — ECRH accessibility

Heating access is a separate closed-form kernel in the same crate: given a
wave frequency plus the caller's magnetic-field and density profiles
tabulated along a beam path, it reports where the wave meets the cyclotron
resonance and where it meets a cut-off — no ray tracing, no launcher
design. The cold electron gyrofrequency (Hutchinson, *Principles of Plasma
Diagnostics*, 2nd ed. (2002), §7.2) is

$$
f_{ce} = \frac{e\,B}{2\pi\,m_e} \approx 27.99249\ \mathrm{GHz/T},
\tag{ECRH-1}
$$

and fundamental (harmonic $n$) absorption sits where $f = n\,f_{ce}$, i.e.

$$
B_{res} = \frac{2\pi\,m_e\,f}{n\,e},
\tag{ECRH-2}
$$

the cold resonance condition pinned by the ECRH review of Bornatici et
al.<Citation id="bornatici-1983" />. At finite electron temperature the
resonance carries the Lorentz factor, $f = n\,f_{ce}/\gamma$, so the layer
moves out to

$$
B_{res}(T_e) = \gamma\,B_{res}(\mathrm{cold}),
\qquad
\gamma = 1 + \frac{T_e}{510.999\ \mathrm{keV}},
\tag{ECRH-3}
$$

the weakly-relativistic Maxwell–Jüttner downshift (Bornatici et
al.<Citation id="bornatici-1983" />). The O-mode cut-off ($P = 0$ of the
cold Appleton–Hartree dispersion; Stix, *Waves in Plasmas* (1992), Ch. 1)
sits at the plasma frequency,

$$
n_{c,O1} = \frac{\varepsilon_0\,m_e\,(2\pi\,f)^2}{e^2},
\tag{ECRH-4}
$$

and the X-mode right-hand cut-off inverts to

$$
n_{c,X1} = n_{c,O1}\,\frac{f - f_{ce}}{f},
\qquad \text{exists only for } f > f_{ce},
\tag{ECRH-5}
$$

(Stix 1992, Ch. 1 — the low-field-side launch cut-off). Where
$f \le f_{ce}$ the X1 wave is evanescent and the kernel returns no
cut-off, never a number. Beamline locators take caller profiles at
strictly increasing positions $s$ [m] and return crossing positions [m] by
linear interpolation between bracketing nodes; exact nodal touches are
reported. Scalar inputs are GHz / T / m⁻³ / keV; the per-port absorbed
power itself lives downstream (blanket bookkeeping consumes these
positions as plain floats).

Pinned hand vectors (170 GHz, ITER-class, $10^{-9}$ relative): cold
resonance $B_{res} = 6.07306$ T at $n = 1$ ($3.03653$ T at $n = 2$);
$\gamma(20\ \mathrm{keV}) = 1.03914$ shifts it to $6.31075$ T; O1 cut-off
$3.58488\times 10^{20}$ m⁻³; X1 cut-off at 5 T $6.33418\times 10^{19}$
m⁻³ — while 100 GHz at 5 T ($f_{ce} = 139.96$ GHz) is evanescent. Beamline
vectors: a 7→5 T fall over $s = 0..2$ m crosses the cold resonance at
$0.92694$ m; a $10^{20}$→$5\times 10^{20}$ m⁻³ rise crosses the 140 GHz O1
cut-off at $0.71563$ m.

## Algorithm

**Ring and point sampling.** A point source sits at its configured position; a
ring source draws a uniform azimuth on the midplane circle, so radius and height
are exact for every particle and only the azimuth carries sampling error. Energies
are the monoenergetic line or a Box–Muller Gaussian draw ($\bar{E} + \sigma z$);
directions are isotropic, with $\cos\theta$ uniform in $[-1, 1]$ and azimuth
uniform in $[0, 2\pi)$.

**Parametric sampling.** At construction the sampler tabulates the
strength-weighted radial distribution on a fixed 256-cell grid over
$[0, a_\text{minor}]$; each cell precomputes its poloidal cumulative distribution
on 256 points (trapezoid quadrature of the periodic $R \cdot |J|$ integrand), so
drawing a particle needs no per-particle quadrature: $r$ from the radial table,
$\theta$ from the owning cell's poloidal table, $\varphi$ uniform, then $(R, Z)$
from the Miller map. The birth energy comes from the Ballabio Gaussian at the
local ion temperature $T_i(r)$. Both samplers run on a small self-contained RNG
(SplitMix64 seed expansion feeding xoshiro256\*\*), so a pinned seed reproduces
the stream on a given platform.

**Tail sampling.** With a deuterium hot tail set, the per-particle branch
choice runs over the five bulk/tail sub-lines weighted by the sub-rate rule
(S5-T) — one extra draw per particle on the mixture path, none when
$\eta = 0$, so the no-tail stream is preserved exactly.

**Lattice sampling.** A lattice particle picks its birth node from the
rate-weighted discrete distribution, then (with symmetry set) its
field-period copy from the within-bin fraction of the same single draw — no
extra draws on any path, so the folded stream reproduces the expanded stream
exactly. Energies follow the node's Ballabio line (or its mixture branch
choice); directions are isotropic as above.

**Source cards and the emission report.** A monoenergetic spectrum renders as a
single line with probability 1. A Gaussian renders as discrete lines at bin
centers over a symmetric $\bar{E} \pm 4\sigma$ window (21 lines by default), with
probabilities from the exact normal CDF (`erf` via the Abramowitz–Stegun 7.1.26
rational approximation, $|\epsilon| \le 1.5\mathrm{e}{-7}$, far below the
six-significant-digit card format); the probability mass outside the window is
dropped and reported. Parametric cards can only carry the *marginals* — radial
and vertical birth histograms plus the global marginal energy spectrum — so the
emission report also carries the joint-correlation distance
$\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}|p_\text{true}(i,j) - p_r(i)\,p_z(j)|$, half the L1 distance
between the true $(r, z)$ birth joint and the product of the marginals (0 for
independent). Every report row records the fraction of the source strength the
card accounts for and the fraction lost (`rel_drift`); `reparsed` marks rows
machine-verified by feeding the emitted text back through that code's own reader
— true for `SDEF`, which must round-trip byte-identically through
`nucleide-mcnp-io` (a failed round trip raises a clear error rather than shipping
unverified text), and false by design for Serpent `src`, where the workspace has
no source reader and the rows are checked analytically against the public
input-manual spelling.

## Worked example: ITER-like D-T ring

An ITER-like D-T ring at $R = 300$ cm, $z = 25$ cm with $T_i = 20$ keV. First the
spectrum and reactivity closed forms through `spectrum_moments` (the Ballabio
moments of a reaction at an ion temperature) and `reactivity` (the Bosch–Hale
$\langle\sigma v\rangle$):

```python
import nucleide.plasma_source as ps

ps.spectrum_moments("dt", 20.0)
# {'reaction': 'dt', 'label': 'D-T', 'nominal_mev': 14.021,
#  'mean_mev': 14.072874253353246, 'sigma_mev': 0.33772176292161377}
ps.spectrum_moments("dd", 0.0)["sigma_mev"]  # 0.0 — cold spectrum is monoenergetic
ps.reactivity("dt", 20.0)                    # 4.330220397689176e-22 m^3/s
ps.reactivity("dd", 20.0)                    # 2.6025829587215236e-24 m^3/s
```

Evaluating (S1a)–(S1b) at $T_i = 20$ keV with the D-T Table III coefficients
gives mean 14.0729 MeV and $\sigma$ 0.33772 MeV against the 14.021 MeV nominal
line; at $T_i = 0$ the width vanishes and both reactions collapse to their
monoenergetic lines (14.021 MeV and 2.4495 MeV). The reactivity ratio at
20 keV, $\langle\sigma v\rangle_{DT} / \langle\sigma v\rangle_{DD} \approx 166$,
is why D-T dominates neutron production at fusion temperatures.

Then sampling and card emission through `particles` (a seeded particle vector)
and `emit_source_cards` (both cards plus the emission report):

```python
import numpy as np
import nucleide.plasma_source as ps

spec = {"kind": "ring", "radius": 300.0, "height": 25.0,
        "reaction": "dt", "ion_temperature_kev": 20.0}
out = ps.particles(spec, 4, seed=2026)
np.hypot(out["x"], out["y"])  # array([300., 300., 300., 300.]) — exactly R
out["z"]                      # array([25., 25., 25., 25.])
```

precision, height exactly 25 cm), so the azimuth is the only sampled spatial
coordinate. The first particle's energy, 14.1785 MeV, sits $0.31\sigma$ above
the 14.0729 MeV mean. Re-running with the same seed replays the identical

card keeps the ring exactly (`RAD=D1` with `SI1 L 300 300`) and tabulates the
Gaussian at 21 bin centers spanning $\bar{E} \pm 4\sigma$ (12.7863–15.3594 MeV).

## Assumptions and limitations

- **Toroidal sectors.** Partial-torus sources take `start_angle`/`rotation_angle`
  (birth angles uniform over the sector; strength scales by
  `rotation/2π`); full rotation reproduces the full-torus kernel exactly.
  Out-of-range angles raise a clear error — never a silent guess.
- **Maxwellian fuel, several choices — plus one pinned tail.** Reactants are Maxwellian: equimolar D-T
  (strength factor $1/4$), pure D-D ($1/2$), a D/T mixture
  `{"D": f_D, "T": f_T}` weighted by the two-branch rate (S5-M), or a mixture
  with per-species ion temperatures `{"D": T_D, "T": T_T}` (the D-T branch
  reacts at the mass-weighted effective temperature) — the equimolar, pure,
  and shared-temperature limits recover the simpler kernels exactly. A
  `deuterium_tail` fraction `{"fraction": η, "temperature_kev": T_tail}`
  adds the one pinned non-Maxwellian shape (S5-T: bulk plus hot tail with
  per-sub-pair lines); `η = 0` recovers the no-tail kernel exactly, and the
  tail needs a `fuel` mixture. Fuel
  fractions that are negative, non-finite, or do not sum to 1 raise a clear
  error, as do negative temperatures and out-of-range tail parameters.
  General non-Maxwellian distributions beyond this shape stay out.
- **Neutron branches plus proton bookkeeping.** The sampler and the emitted
  cards are neutron-only (D-T + D-D lines; no T-T neutron branch — a pure-T
  mixture is a loud error). The D(d,p)T proton branch is reported as
  bookkeeping next to the source (the proton density is the D-D
  neutron-branch density, pinned 50/50); proton transport is out of scope.
- **Profiles are caller inputs.** Nothing computes or fits profiles and no
  equilibrium is solved. Malformed geometry or profile parameters (non-positive
  minor radius or elongation, triangularity outside $(-1, 1)$, pedestal radius
  outside $(0, a)$, non-positive centre density or temperature) raise a clear,
  named error, and a configuration whose total emission strength is zero raises
  a clear error instead of sampling nothing.
- **Lattice clouds are caller inputs.** Nothing computes the 3D birth
  distribution and no equilibrium is read: node positions, rates, and ion
  temperatures arrive as data (a point list, not a grid). Empty clouds,
  negative rates, zero totals, and non-symmetric fold inputs raise clear,
  named errors.
- **Published fit domains.** The Ballabio and Bosch–Hale fits are used within
  their published ranges. Negative or non-finite ion temperatures raise a clear
  error, and the D-D reactivity's correction factor goes non-positive on
  roughly 965–2720 keV, where the crate raises a clear out-of-domain error rather than
  returning NaN.
- **Cards carry marginals, not correlations.** A product-form card cannot
  represent the correlated $(r, z)$ birth joint or the position–energy
  correlation; the emission report quantifies both losses (the truncated energy
  tail and the joint-correlation distance).
- **Determinism per platform.** A pinned seed reproduces the particle stream on
  a given platform; platform libm differences can shift the last ulp of
  individual streams, with no effect on moments or card text at any meaningful
  tolerance.
- **No file writing in the crate.** Particle vectors and card strings are
  returned to the caller; writing MCPL or MCNP files stays caller-side
  (`nucleide.mcpl`, `nucleide.mcnp`).
- **ECRH reports positions, never designs launchers.** Resonance and
  cut-off crossings are plain floats for downstream bookkeeping; ray
  tracing, beam optics, and absorbed-power computation stay out. An
  evanescent X1 reports no cut-off rather than a number, and empty,
  ragged, or non-monotonic beamlines raise a clear error.

## Validation

- **Rust unit tests** pin the transcriptions: spectrum moments and reactivities
  at 1/10/20 keV recomputed independently from the published coefficients, the
  Miller closed forms (torus limit, the $\delta = 0$ area and volume identities,
  finite-difference agreement of the Jacobian at general $\delta$), profile
  definitions and pedestal continuity, sampler determinism, and a pinned
  four-particle stream for the RNG.
- **`validation/plasma_source_vs_openmc.py`** rebuilds every quantity from a
  second, independent in-script transcription of the published formulas.
  Always-on checks cover ring geometry and azimuth moments, Ballabio moments
  (< 1e-12 relative), pinned-seed determinism, the `SDEF` round trip, the
  tabulation tail drift against the $\pm 4\sigma$ tail mass, sampled
  flat-profile moments against the $\delta = 0$ closed forms
  ($\langle r^2\rangle = a^2/2$, $\langle z\rangle = 0$, and the outboard
  volume bias $\langle R\rangle = R_0 + a^2/(4R_0)$), the reactivity
  transcription (< 1e-12), and H-mode sampled birth moments against independent
  fine quadrature of $S\,R\,|J|$, plus the P12 lattice gates (two-point hand
  vectors, single-point recovery, ring-limit closure, symmetry-fold
  replication, card round trip, mixture branch fire) and the P13 hot-tail
  gates (sub-rate-rule moments and branch share, the drift tail-neutron
  share, zero-fraction recovery, loud errors).
- **Container-only cross-checks** against the MIT-licensed
  `openmc-plasma-source` package and NeSST (Ballabio helpers, sampled D-D and
  ring moments, the Miller map, L/H profiles, reactivities to ~5e-9 — the
  published coefficients' own precision — end-to-end parametric moments, the
  lattice ring-limit probe, and the hot-tail blend probe).
  When the packages are unavailable these checks are reported as SKIP with the
  reason, never silently.
- **ECRH hand vectors** in `ecrh.rs` unit tests and
  `tests/test_ecrh_access.py` pin the closed forms at $10^{-9}$ relative
  (the 170 GHz resonance/shift/cut-off vectors above plus beamline
  crossings and loud-error vectors). ECRH has no oracle leg: closed forms
  need no cross-code leg, only the two independent transcriptions.
- **`tests/test_plasma_source.py`** replays the user-facing Python surface.

## Related work beyond Nucleide

Adjacent capabilities Nucleide does not implement: the Eriksson et al.
generalization beyond the one pinned single-tail-temperature shape —
arbitrary non-Maxwellian reactant distributions, which covers
beam-target spectra beyond the hot-tail approximation (the documented "not
yet supported"
boundary above)<Citation id="eriksson-2016" />; adaptive Monte Carlo source
iteration in the spirit of KDSource (GPL-licensed, not integrated); OpenMC's
arbitrary mesh- and CAD-based spatial sources — OpenMC's fusion-specific sources
themselves ship in the separate `openmc-plasma-source` package, which the
validation harness cross-checks against at runtime; and
synthetic diagnostic responses beyond the source description itself, the
territory of NeSST.
