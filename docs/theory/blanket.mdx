Blanket studies need TBR bookkeeping, not transport: the tallies come from
the caller's transport runs, and the penalties, multiplications, and
margins below are closed-form arithmetic over those caller values. This
page pins the equation set of `nucleide.blanket` (labels B1–B4, P1–P2,
F1–F2, matching the crate rustdoc) and the reported stellarator Point-A
values that gate it as regression vectors.

## Overview

A breeding blanket must make at least one triton per source neutron
(breakeven, TBR = 1) with margin to cover losses, ports, and uncertainties.
The kernel takes caller-tallied `(bred, source)` pairs plus port coverage
fractions, power levels, and a required TBR, and returns the effective TBR,
the margin, the blanket power, the tritium burn rate, and the net surplus —
plain floats, never a geometry or a target solve.

<Callout type="info" title="Code shape">
  The `nucleide-blanket` crate is one kernel file plus errors: `lib.rs`
  (the B1–F2 arithmetic with the Stellaris regression gates) and `error.rs`
  (a `#[non_exhaustive]` error enum with the crate `Result` alias). The
  Python surface (`nucleide.blanket`) is thin. There is no validation oracle
  script: no external oracle exists for bookkeeping arithmetic, so the gates
  below replay analytically in-crate and in `tests/test_blanket.py`.
</Callout>

## Mathematical details

### B1 — Raw TBR

$$
\mathrm{TBR} = \frac{\mathrm{bred}}{\mathrm{source}},
\tag{B1}
$$

dimensionless, with `bred` the caller-tallied tritons bred (non-negative)
and `source` the source neutrons (strictly positive).

### B2 — Port-penalty haircut

Each port (ECRH launcher, diagnostics, maintenance) removes blanket
coverage; the haircut is multiplicative over the per-port fractions
$f_i \in [0, 1)$:

$$
\mathrm{TBR_{eff}} = \mathrm{TBR_{raw}}\prod_i (1 - f_i).
\tag{B2}
$$

An empty port list returns the raw TBR unchanged. The ECRH accessibility
positions on the [fusion-sources page](fusion-sources.mdx#s7--ecrh-accessibility)
are the per-port penalty inputs.

### B3–B4 — Breeding margin and requirement check

$$
\mathrm{margin} = \mathrm{TBR_{eff}} - 1,
\tag{B3}
$$

$$
\mathrm{TBR_{eff}} \ge \mathrm{TBR_{required}}.
\tag{B4}
$$

A negative margin is a sub-breakeven deficit and is reported as-is, never
clamped.

### P1–P2 — Energy multiplication and blanket power

$$
M = \frac{P_\mathrm{blanket}}{P_\mathrm{fusion}},
\qquad
P_\mathrm{blanket} = M\,P_\mathrm{fusion}\ \mathrm{[MW]}.
\tag{P1–P2}
$$

### F1–F2 — Tritium burn rate and net surplus

One triton (3.0160492 u) burns per 17.6 MeV of D-T fusion energy, scaled to
grams per day per MW of fusion power:

$$
\mathrm{burn} = P_\mathrm{fusion}\,K\ \mathrm{[g/day]}.
$$

$$
K = \frac{10^6 \cdot 86400 \cdot (3.0160492\,\mathrm{u\ in\ kg}) \cdot 10^3}
{17.6\,\mathrm{MeV\ in\ J}} = 0.153454\ \mathrm{g/day\ per\ MW},
\tag{F1}
$$

with the SI-exact atomic mass unit and electronvolt; the crate spells the
constant pair plus the SI conversions as documented `pub const` values.
The net fuel-cycle surplus is the margin times the burn:

$$
\mathrm{net} = (\mathrm{TBR_{eff}} - 1)\,\mathrm{burn}\ \mathrm{[g/day]},
\tag{F2}
$$

negative when sub-breakeven (a net deficit, never clamped).

## Algorithm

Validate first (finite inputs, non-negative tallies, strictly positive
source neutrons, port fractions in $[0, 1)$), then evaluate (B1)–(F2) in
one pass — each metric is a pure function of its inputs with no shared
state, so callers compose them in any order.

## Worked example: Point-A anchors

The reported stellarator Point-A values gate the arithmetic as regression
vectors (gates, not inputs): raw TBR $1.1070$ at exact equality, $1.07379$
after the 3% ECRH-port haircut ($1.1070 \times 0.97$, report print
$1.074$), multiplication $1.20$, and burn $416.6$ g/day:

```python
import nucleide.blanket as blk

blk.tbr_from_tallies(1.1070, 1.0)          # 1.107 — (B1)
blk.apply_port_penalty(1.1070, [0.03])     # 1.07379 — (B2)
blk.breeding_margin(1.07379)               # 0.07379 — (B3)
blk.blanket_power(2714.82, 1.20)           # 3257.784 MW — (P2)
blk.tritium_burn_g_per_day(2714.82)        # 416.6 g/day — (F1)
blk.net_surplus_g_per_day(1.07379, 2714.82)  # ~30.74 g/day — (F2)
```

Hand checks: $0.153454 \times 2714.82 = 416.60$ (the 2714.82 MW Point-A
fusion power is the power implied by 416.6 g/day through (F1), not a
separate published input — the published anchor is the 416.6 value
itself), and $(1.07379 - 1) \times 416.6 = 30.74$ g/day net surplus.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **Arithmetic only.** No transport solving, no TBR target solving, no
  geometry optimization — and no coupling into the `tritium` permeation
  kernel, which keeps its no-breeding-coupling scope line; this companion
  owns the breeding-side arithmetic.
- **Gates, not inputs.** The Point-A values pin the implementation; every
  real input (tallies, port fractions, powers, required TBR) is caller
  data, validated loudly (non-finite, negative, non-positive-source, and
  out-of-range port fractions are named errors).
- **Deficits stay negative.** Sub-breakeven margins and net results are
  reported as-is so callers can size the shortfall; nothing clamps at zero.

## Validation

- **Rust unit tests** replay the Point-A anchors (raw TBR exact, penalized
  TBR, multiplication, burn at the implied power, margin/surplus gates)
  plus the burn-constant identity and loud-input vectors.
- **`tests/test_blanket.py`** replays the same anchors through the Python
  surface with an independent transcription of the (F1) burn constant
  (grouped differently from the Rust const expression) at $10^{-12}$
  relative.
- **No oracle script**: bookkeeping arithmetic has no external oracle, so
  there is no `validation/*_vs_*.py` leg and no `SECTION_ORDER` entry —
  the analytic gates above are the contract.

## Related work beyond Nucleide

The tallies this kernel consumes come from transport codes (OpenMC/DAGMC
blanket runs); the port-penalty inputs come from the ECRH accessibility
kernel on the fusion-sources page. What Nucleide does not implement:
blanket neutronics itself, TBR target solving, and tritium transport
(the `tritium` kernel's documented scope line).
