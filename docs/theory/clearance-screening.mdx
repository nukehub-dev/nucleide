Activation codes such as ALARA and FISPACT-II answer "how radioactive is this
material after irradiation and cooling?". Clearance screening asks the
follow-up regulatory question: "is it low enough to be released from
regulatory control?". Nucleide implements the screening arithmetic — the
**clearance index** and the **sum-of-fractions rule** — as pure arithmetic
over parsed activation inventories, with the EU clearance table vendored as an
attributed default. This is screening arithmetic, never a compliance
decision: real clearance requires the governing regulatory table, material
bookkeeping, and the national transposition of the underlying directive.

## Overview

A clearance table lists, per radionuclide, an activity concentration below
which material may be cleared from regulatory control. Given an inventory of
per-nuclide activities `A_i` and matching per-nuclide clearance levels
`CL_i`, each nuclide contributes the fraction `A_i / CL_i` of its limit, and
the material satisfies the screening criterion when the sum of those
fractions does not exceed 1 — the **sum-of-fractions rule** of IAEA Safety
Standards Series No. RS-G-1.7 §5, referenced here by designation only (the
IAEA tables themselves are never vendored). The methodology follows Sublet
et al.<Citation id="sublet-2017" />

Nucleide keeps the arithmetic table-driven and the inputs pluggable:

- **Limit tables** are caller-supplied key/value maps, or the vendored EU
  2013/59/Euratom Annex VII Table A transcription used as the attributed
  default<Citation id="eu-2013-59-euratom" />.
- **Inventories** come from parsed activation output — ALARA response-frame
  rows (Rust `inventory_from_frame`), or any other source supplying the same
  `(nuclide, activity)` pairs — and from the FISPACT-II wide inventory table
  printed with the `HAZARDS` + `CLEAR` keywords, whose per-nuclide clearance
  index column Nucleide can read back verbatim.
- **Classification** returns the fraction sum, a satisfied/exceeded class,
  and the dominant contributor — the single nuclide carrying the largest
  fraction.

## Mathematical details

### Clearance index

For an inventory of `N` nuclides with activities `A_i` and clearance levels
`CL_i` on a common unit basis, the per-nuclide fraction and the clearance
index are

$$
f_i = \frac{A_i}{CL_i}, \qquad
\mathrm{CI} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} f_i = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{A_i}{CL_i}.
\tag{CI}
$$

### Sum-of-fractions rule

The screening classification places the boundary at `CI = 1`, boundary
included — matching the exemption/clearance wording "does not exceed":

$$
\mathrm{CI} \le 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{screening satisfied},
\qquad
\mathrm{CI} > 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{screening exceeded}.
\tag{SOF}
$$

The same pass reports the dominant contributor, the nuclide whose `f_i` is
the largest single fraction (useful for finding what to wait out or clean
up); an empty inventory gives `CI = 0` with no dominant nuclide.

### The vendored EU default table

returns a transcription of Council Directive 2013/59/Euratom Annex VII

radionuclides … for solid materials" — in Bq/g (numerically identical to the

### The FISPACT-II clearance column

In the wide `HAZARDS` + `CLEAR` inventory table, FISPACT-II prints a
per-nuclide clearance-index column computed code-side as the activity divided
by the code's own internal clearance-library value — not against the EU
table. Nucleide's reader carries that column verbatim (and can total it), so
code-printed and Nucleide-recomputed indices stay distinguishable; the
screening functions themselves always recompute `f_i` against the caller's
table.

### Sublet S1+S2 radiological totals

Inventories also feed two radiological totals, pinned to the open
CCFE-PR(16)53 author preprint of the FISPACT-II system paper — Tables X
(inventory/power row definitions), VI (decay-type identifiers), VII
(radiation types), and XVII (validation anchors) — and the open FISPACT-II
`output_interpretation` reference, never the paywalled journal pages:

$$
A_i = N_i\,\lambda_i\ \mathrm{[Bq]},
\qquad
A_\mathrm{tot} = \sum_i A_i,
\tag{S1}
$$

with the FISPACT-II IRT α/β/γ split from the `output_interpretation`
activity-breakdown section: IRT 4 → alpha; IRT 1, 2, 11, 14, 16, 17, 19,
20 → beta; IRT 3 → gamma; IRT 12, 13 split α/β and IRT 15 splits α/γ by
caller-supplied branch fractions (IRT classes are Table VI, identifiers
1–27 with 8, 9 unused and 10 unknown; unmapped IRTs are a clear error).
Decay heat folds caller-supplied average decay energies into per-class
power:

$$
H_c = \sum_i A_i\,E_{c,i}\,C_1\ \mathrm{[kW]},
\qquad
C_1 = 1.602176634\times 10^{-22}\ \mathrm{kJ/eV},
\tag{S2}
$$

the Table X `Ai·E·C1` rows (radiation classes are Table VII, STYP 0–9).
Both totals carry the excluding-tritium companion (total minus the
tritium entry), and parts sum to the total exactly by construction.
Decay energies stay caller-supplied, never vendored; S3–S7, dose-rate and
ingestion/inhalation indices, bremsstrahlung, DPA/KERMA/gas, and fission
counts stay out. Gates are hand-computed vectors at exact equality
(Table XVII copper total-activity anchor) plus always-run C7 gates in the
same `clearance` validation report.

## Algorithm

- **Table construction.** `ClearanceTable` is a canonical-`NuclideId`-keyed
  map. `insert` validates that each limit is finite and strictly positive and
  raises a clear error otherwise. The EU default is embedded as a TSV at
  build time, parsed lazily on first call, and its invariants are checked at
  load — since it is committed data, a corrupt transcription fails loudly
  rather than returning a partial table. Nuclide keys resolve through the
  shared dialect machinery, so `Co60`, `Co-60`, and `co-60` are one key, not
  three.
- **Screening accumulation.** One pass over the inventory: validate each
  activity (finite, `>= 0`), require a table entry for every inventory
  nuclide (a missing limit raises a clear error — never a silent skip),
  accumulate `f_i` into the sum, and track the running maximum fraction and
  its nuclide. The class falls out of (SOF) with the boundary included.
- **ALARA frame inventories.** `inventory_from_frame` keeps the
  `SpecificActivity` rows at one cooling time and drops the `total`
  aggregate row, yielding `(nuclide, activity)` pairs in the frame's own
  activity unit (ALARA prints Bq/cm³, FISPACT-II prints Bq) — the caller
  pairs them with a table on the matching basis.
- **FISPACT-II wide-table reader.** `parse_clearance` recognizes
  `TIME INTERVAL <n>` headers with their `TIME IS` / `COOLING TIME IS` step
  tags (the first `<value> <unit>` pair gives the step time in seconds, with
  `s m h d w y` names accepted and `y = 365 d`), then the two-line
  `NUCLIDE … CLEARANCE … HALF LIFE` column header, then data rows of
  `<symbol> <mass>[isomer] [flags] <11 floats> <half-life|Stable>`. Flags
  (`#` stable, `>` present before irradiation, `&` gamma spectrum
  approximate, `?` convergence not reached) are kept verbatim, `Stable` is
  stored as `-1.0` seconds (matching the ALARA output convention), and
  nuclide names are normalized to the shared dialect spelling (`V 55` →
  `V-55`, `Rb 86m` → `Rb-86m`). The table ends at `TOTAL NUMBER OF NUCLIDES
  PRINTED IN INVENTORY` or the first non-data line; blank input and every
  malformed row or header raise a clear, line-numbered parse error, while a
  file with no clearance table at all yields an empty scan for the caller to
  judge.

## Worked example

**Hand arithmetic.** Take the vendored EU defaults, `CL_Co = 0.1 Bq/g` and
`CL_H = 100 Bq/g`, and a massic inventory `A_Co = 0.05 Bq/g`,
`A_H = 25 Bq/g`. Then `f_Co = 0.05/0.1 = 0.5`, `f_H = 25/100 = 0.25`, and
`CI = 0.75 ≤ 1` — satisfied, with Co-60 the dominant contributor at half the
limit on its own.

**Real API, end to end.** The same screen over a parsed FISPACT-II clearance
table (the synthetic fixture `fixtures/fispact/clearance.out`, interval 1:
stable V-55 and Fe-56 at zero activity, Co-60 at 4.0e6 Bq, Rb-86m at
8.0e9 Bq) against caller limits `{V-55: 1, Fe-56: 1, Co-60: 4.0e6, Rb-86m:
8.0e9}` gives `f = 0 + 0 + 1 + 1 = 2.0 > 1` — exceeded:

```python
from pathlib import Path

import nucleide.alara as alara
import nucleide.fispact as fispact

# Two-nuclide inventory against the vendored EU default table (Bq/g).
out = alara.alara_sum_of_fractions({"Co-60": 0.05, "H-3": 25.0})
print(out)

# Same screen over a parsed FISPACT-II clearance table.
rows = fispact.fispact_parse_clearance(Path("fixtures/fispact/clearance.out").read_text())
step1 = {r["nuclide"]: r["activity_bq"] for r in rows if r["interval"] == 1}
print(alara.alara_sum_of_fractions(step1, {"V-55": 1.0, "Fe-56": 1.0,
                                           "Co-60": 4.0e6, "Rb-86m": 8.0e9}))
```

```text
{'class': 'satisfied', 'max_fraction': 0.5, 'max_nuclide': 'Co-60', 'sum': 0.75}
{'class': 'exceeded', 'max_fraction': 1.0, 'max_nuclide': 'Co-60', 'sum': 2.0}
```

Both vectors are hand-computable exactly, and the sum-of-fractions boundary
is probed on both sides in the test suite: `CI = 1` classifies as satisfied,
one float-ulp above as exceeded.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **Screening arithmetic, not a compliance decision.** Clearance in practice
  requires the governing regulatory table, complete material bookkeeping,
  and the national transposition of the underlying directive.
- **Unit basis is the caller's discipline.** `A_i` and `CL_i` must share one
  basis. The vendored EU table is an activity-*concentration* table (Bq/g),
  so inventories screened against it must be massic activities (Bq/g), not
  total activities (Bq). Frame extraction returns activities in the frame's
  own unit (ALARA Bq/cm³ vs FISPACT-II Bq), so the table basis must be
  chosen to match.
- **Complete tables, valid values.** Every inventory nuclide must have a
  table entry; a missing limit raises a clear error. Negative or non-finite
  activities, and non-positive or non-finite limits, raise a clear error —
  nothing is skipped silently. Zero activities contribute nothing.
- **Duplicate nuclides** in a caller-built inventory (Rust slice input) sum
  like any other pair — deduplication is the caller's job; Python dict keys
  are unique by construction.
- **The FISPACT-II clearance column is code-side.** Its per-row values are
  the code's internal library ratios, not EU-table fractions, and are read
  back as printed; Nucleide screening always recomputes against the caller's
  table.
- **Further indices stay recorded, not implemented.** Sublet et al.'s wider
  radiological indices (dose rate, ingestion, inhalation, Bq/A2) appear as
  columns in the wide table and are parsed where present, but only the
  clearance screen plus the S1+S2 totals above are implemented as metrics<Citation id="sublet-2017" />.
- **S1+S2 input discipline.** Per-nuclide activities and decay energies are
  caller data (finite, non-negative); the α branch fraction is required for
  the split IRTs 12, 13, 15 and rejected elsewhere; IRTs with no pinned
  mapping raise a clear error, never a silent bucket.
- **Time and stability conventions.** FISPACT-II step times convert with
  `y = 365 d`; `Stable` half-lives store as `-1.0`.
- **Correctness checks.** The arithmetic is pinned by hand-computed vectors
  at exact equality (CI values, boundary probes both sides, EU and ES spot
  values, ES chain-expansion members) and by an end-to-end parse of the
  synthetic fixture in the real FISPACT-II grammar, in
  `tests/test_clearance.py` and `validation/clearance_vs_pypact.py`. The S1+S2
  totals are pinned the same way: hand-computed activity/heat vectors at exact
  equality (parts-to-total conservation, ex-tritium companions, split-IRT
  fractions, loud-error vectors) plus the always-run C7 gates in the same
  report.

## Related work beyond Nucleide

The independent comparison for the reader is **pypact**, the upstream
Apache-2.0 FISPACT-II output reader on PyPI<Citation id="pypact" />: this
repository's validation harness re-parses the synthetic clearance fixture
with `pypact`'s `InventoryReader` and compares the overlapping per-nuclide
columns (atoms, activity, clearance index) — agreement is exact (zero
relative error) over the fixture's seven nuclide-steps, and the check
reports a clearly-stated skip when pypact is not installed. Adjacent
capabilities Nucleide does not implement: the rest of Sublet et al.'s
radiological index suite as first-class metrics, and any vending of the IAEA
clearance/exemption tables (RS-G-1.7, GSG-17), which stay designation-only
references.
