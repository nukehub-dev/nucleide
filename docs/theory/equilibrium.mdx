Equilibrium codes solve the plasma shape; everything downstream — volume
weighting, wall loads, source normalization — only reads their output.
This page pins the data layer of `nucleide-equilib-io`: a pure
classic-netCDF `wout` reader, the VMEC `&INDATA` input-text grammar, the
flux-surface Jacobian helpers (J1–J3), and the wall-load accumulation
kernel (W1–W3). Reads data, never solves equilibria.

## Overview

A VMEC `wout` file stores the equilibrium as Fourier coefficients of the
flux-surface geometry plus scalar parameters. The reader accepts the two
classic-netCDF variants per magic probe — CDF-1 (`43 44 46 01`) and CDF-2
(`43 44 46 02`), the spellings `ncdump -k` reports as `classic` and
`64-bit offset` — and rejects HDF5-backed netCDF-4 and CDF-5 loudly for
facade-side conversion: no HDF5 in Rust, by rule. The documented variable
minimum for Jacobians is `nfp`, `ns`, `xm`, `xn`, `rmnc`, `zmns`, `lmns`,
`gmnc` on dims `radius`/`mn_mode`/`mn_mode_nyq` (later-use field/current
tables ride through verbatim; `mpol`/`ntor`/`phiedge`/`volume_p` are
optional scalars). The `&INDATA` grammar covers scalar switches,
power-series profiles, and boundary Fourier tables (`NCURR = 1` reads as
the `I'(s)` profile; absent `LFREEB` reads fixed boundary; sliced indices
are a loud error).

## Mathematical details

### J1 — Flux-surface Jacobian

The Jacobian $\sqrt{g}$ is the `gmnc` Fourier sum over the resolved
Nyquist mode maps:

$$
\sqrt{g}(\theta, \zeta) = \sum c\,\cos(m\,\theta - n\,\zeta),
\tag{J1}
$$

with $\zeta$ following the file's toroidal convention (one field period —
the caller scales totals for full-torus work). Nyquist maps resolve from
`xm_nyq`/`xn_nyq` when present, else from `xm`/`xn` when their length
already matches `mn_mode_nyq`; anything else fails loudly instead of
guessing a truncation.

### J2–J3 — Grids and surface average

J2 grids `ntheta` points over $[0, 2\pi)$ by `nzeta` over
$[0, 2\pi/\mathrm{nfp})$ for volume weighting; J3 reads the $(0, 0)$
coefficient (loud when absent). The free `fourier_jacobian` kernel stays
caller-array-clean for the wall-load consumer below.

### W1–W3 — Wall-load mapping

A caller birth-rate density field maps onto the caller wall surface
sharing the same flux coordinates ($s$ the normalized toroidal-flux label,
$\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$ poloidal, $\zeta$ toroidal over one field period;
radial-major voxels). Each wall node sees the radial line integral at its
own angles:

$$
q[j][k] = \sum_i S[i][j][k]\,J[i][j][k]\,\Delta s[i],
\tag{W1}
$$

with $S$ the birth-rate density, $J = \sqrt{g}$, and $\Delta s[i]$ the
$i$-th radial cell width. The angle-weighted one-field-period total
conserves the discrete births to roundoff by construction:

$$
Q = \sum_{jk} q[j][k]\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta\zeta
= \sum_{ijk} S\,J\,\Delta s\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta\zeta.
\tag{W2}
$$

The axisymmetric limit is analytic: uniform $S$, uniform $J = J_0$ give the
spatially constant wall flux $q = S\,J_0\,(s_{hi} - s_{lo})$ over the
radial span (W3). Pure glue — no transport, no shadowing, no FEM/thermal,
no CAD; shape mismatches and non-finite or negative densities/Jacobians
are loud errors reusing the crate vocabulary.

## Algorithm

Probe the magic first (accept two variants, reject the rest with the
conversion remedy), walk the classic header into named fields, resolve
mode maps, then evaluate sums: Fourier sums for Jacobians, the (W1) line
integral per wall node plus the (W2) total for wall loads, single-pass
namelist scan for `&INDATA`.

## Worked example: uniform-field wall load

One radial cell over $s \in [0, 1]$, uniform birth density $S = 2.0$,
uniform Jacobian $J_0 = 1.5$, four poloidal by two toroidal nodes at two
field periods:

```python
import nucleide.equilib as eq

out = eq.wall_load([0.0, 1.0], 4, 2, 2, [2.0] * 8, [1.5] * 8)
out["loads"]  # [[3.0, 3.0]] * 4 — every node sees S·J0·Δs = 3.0 (W3)
out["total"]  # 59.21762640653615 — 8 nodes × 3.0 × (π/2)·(π/2) = 6π² (W2)
```

Hand checks: (W1) gives $2.0 \times 1.5 \times 1.0 = 3.0$ at every node;
(W2) weights by $\Delta\theta = 2\pi/4 = \pi/2$ and
$\Delta\zeta = 2\pi/(2 \cdot 2) = \pi/2$, so
$Q = 8 \times 3.0 \times \pi^2/4 = 6\pi^2$.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **Reads, never solves.** No equilibrium solving or optimization, no coil
  design; DESC-saved files and VMEC++-era outputs stay out until
  re-probed (every file is probed regardless).
- **Classic only in Rust.** CDF-1/CDF-2 are accepted; netCDF-4/HDF5 and
  CDF-5 fail loudly for facade-side conversion. Synthetic test bytes are
  hand-packed from the published variable lists, never solver output.
- **One-field-period convention.** $\zeta$ grids and wall-load totals cover
  $[0, 2\pi/\mathrm{nfp})$; full-torus scaling is the caller's multiply.
- **Wall-load scope.** Caller densities, caller Jacobians, caller wall
  grid — the kernel is the accumulation math plus gates. A zero Jacobian
  is allowed (null-volume voxel); a negative one is not.

## Validation

- **Rust unit tests** pin magic-byte accept/reject vectors, synthetic
  CDF-1/CDF-2 round trips, Jacobian hand vectors, `&INDATA` grammar
  vectors, and the wall-load gates (axisymmetric analytic limit vs the
  $S \cdot J_0$ flux with its closed-form total, cosine-Jacobian vector,
  radial weighting, discrete conservation) at $10^{-12}$ relative.
- **`tests/test_equilib.py`** replays the Python surface, including the
  worked example above.
- **`validation/equilib_vs_desc.py`** replays the gates as always-run E9–E10
  rows in the same `equilib` report; the DESC/simsopt cross-check legs SKIP
  loudly outside the container (optional oracle dependencies recorded in
  `validation/environment.json`).

## Related work beyond Nucleide

The file formats originate with VMEC<Citation id="hirshman-whitson-1983" />;
the DESC<Citation id="dudt-kolemen-2020" /> and
simsopt<Citation id="landreman-2021" /> packages are
runtime cross-check oracles only (never ported). What Nucleide does not
implement: equilibrium solving, VMEC++-era outputs, stellarator
optimization, and anything CAD, FEM, or HDF5-shaped in Rust.
