Nucleide implements neutron spectrum unfolding from activation-type
measurements: a caller-supplied guess spectrum is adjusted until folding it
through a caller-supplied response matrix reproduces the measured
detector/reaction rates. Four methods share one iterator and diagnostics
shape, landed one per cycle: SAND-II (Spectrum Analysis by Neutron
Detectors II; McElroy et al., AFWL-TR-67-41, 1967)<Citation id="mcelroy-1967" />,
written clean-room from the public-domain report — the adjustment form
reproduced across the open unfolding literature; STAYSL-class damped
least-squares (Perey, ORNL/TM-6062, 1977); GRAVEL chi-square-weighted
adjustment (Matzke, PTB-N-19, 1994); and MAXED maximum entropy
(Reginatto & Goldhagen, Health Phys. 77 (1999) 579)<Citation id="reginatto-1999" />,
from the journal equations only — the closed UMG package is never touched.
Every response value,
measured rate, guess entry, and energy-group bound is caller-supplied data:
evaluated libraries such as IRDFF are IAEA-copyright and are never vendored —
the IRDFF-II response pack below ships as a runtime download, parsed into
caller-ready rows. The IRDFF-II analytical benchmark-field shapes used in the
correctness checks are re-derived from their published closed
forms<Citation id="trkov-2020" /> — the library's tabulated group spectra are
never read. The S1–S3 labels are the SAND-II pinned equations, T1–T4 the
STAYSL-class set, G1–G3 the GRAVEL set, M1–M6 the MAXED set;
U1–U23 label the correctness checks.

## Overview

Foil-activation spectrometry — the JET/ITER diagnostics workhorse — measures a
handful of detector/reaction rates and asks for the neutron spectrum behind
them, one value per energy group. The forward problem is a matrix
multiplication (the *fold*); the inverse problem has no unique answer whenever
there are fewer detectors than energy groups, which is the normal case.
Unfolding therefore does not invent the unseen part: it iteratively adjusts a
caller-supplied guess until the fold reproduces the measurements, moving only
the components the detectors can see. It is inverse-problem iteration, not a
transport solve.

The Python surface `nucleide.unfold` (a thin facade over the crate) exposes
five entry points: `sandii(...)`, `staysl(...)`, `gravel(...)`, and
`maxed(...)`, which run their adjustment and return the converged spectrum
with diagnostics, and `forward_fold(...)`, the shared forward operator.

## Mathematical details

Definitions: a response matrix `R` with one row per detector/reaction (entry
`R_ij ≥ 0` per energy group `j`), measured rates `N_i ≥ 0`, and a guess
spectrum `φ_j > 0` (strictly positive — the update is multiplicative).

### S1 — Forward fold

$$
c_i = \sum_j R_{ij}\,\phi_j
\tag{S1}
$$

The calculated rate `c_i` of detector `i`. This same map is how the
correctness checks synthesize measured rates from a known spectrum.

### S2 — Detector rate-share weights

$$
W_{ji} = R_{ij}\,\phi_j / c_i
\tag{S2}
$$

The share of detector `i`'s calculated rate contributed by group `j`; the
shares sum to one over `j` for each detector with a nonzero fold.

### S3 — Multiplicative adjustment

$$
\phi_j \leftarrow \phi_j \cdot
\exp\!\left( \frac{\sum_i W_{ji}\,\ln(N_i / c_i)}{\sum_i W_{ji}} \right)
\tag{S3}
$$

One adjustment per iteration: the new spectrum is the old one times a weighted
geometric mean of the per-detector correction factors `N_i / c_i`, weighted
by the S2 rate shares. Three properties follow directly:

- **Positivity**: a strictly positive guess stays non-negative — and stays
  strictly positive when all measured rates are positive (a zero measurement
  pins its groups to exactly zero).
- **Fixed point**: a guess that already reproduces the rates is unchanged
  (every correction factor is 1).
- **Unseen groups keep the guess**: a group no detector responds to has
  `Σ_i W_ji = 0`; its adjustment factor is exactly 1.

`W_ji` is the canonical base SAND-II weight. The original implementation's
optional per-detector statistics factor (`N_i²/σ_i`) is not applied, so
SAND-II adjustments are not weighted by detector uncertainty<Citation id="mcelroy-1967" /> —
that weighting is what the GRAVEL sibling below adds.

### T1–T4 — STAYSL-class damped least-squares

Where SAND-II multiplies the guess by correction factors, the STAYSL-class
method adjusts it toward the least-squares fit of the measured rates. The
forward fold is linear, so the objective is the quadratic anchored-damped
form:

$$
c_i = \sum_j R_{ij}\,\phi_j
\tag{T1}
$$

$$
\phi^\dagger = \arg\min_\phi \sum_i w_i\,(c_i(\phi) - N_i)^2
  + \lambda \sum_j (\phi_j - \phi_{\mathrm{guess},j})^2,
  \qquad w_i = 1/\sigma_i^2
\tag{T2}
$$

Each iterator cycle re-solves (T2) about the current iterate as one weighted
least-squares system through the shared `linalg` least-squares kernel —
there is no second least-squares implementation — solving for the increment
`Δ = φ† − φ_k`:

$$
\Delta_A = \arg\min_\Delta \sum_i w_i
  \left(\sum_{j \in A} R_{ij}\,\Delta_j - r_i\right)^2
  + \lambda \sum_{j \in A} (\Delta_j - (\phi_{\mathrm{guess},j} - \phi_{k,j}))^2,
  \qquad r_i = N_i - c_i(\phi_k)
\tag{T3}
$$

with groups the solve drives non-positive clipped to zero and pinned there
for the rest of the run (T4, the active-set convention). Sigmas are
caller-supplied, one finite positive value per detector; anything else
raises a clear error. The damping `λ` is absolute Tikhonov damping on the
distance to the guess: directions the data constrains much stronger than
`λ` fit the rates, directions constrained much weaker keep the guess. Once
a cycle produces no clip and no movement above the tolerance, the next
cycle is a bit-for-bit no-op and the run has converged. Unlike SAND-II the
update is additive rather than multiplicative, and the fixed point
reproduces the rates only up to the damping slack.

### G1–G3 — GRAVEL chi-square weights

GRAVEL is the uncertainty-aware sibling of SAND-II: the same
positivity-preserving multiplicative adjustment, but each detector's
correction is weighted by its measurement precision:

$$
c_i = \sum_j R_{ij}\,\phi_j
\tag{G1}
$$

$$
W_{ji} = \frac{R_{ij}\,\phi_j}{c_i} \cdot \frac{N_i^2}{\sigma_i^2}
\tag{G2}
$$

$$
\phi_j \leftarrow \phi_j \cdot
\exp\!\left( \frac{\sum_i W_{ji}\,\ln(N_i / c_i)}{\sum_i W_{ji}} \right)
\tag{G3}
$$

(G2) is the SAND-II base rate share times the measurement-weight factor
`N_i²/σ_i²`: a precisely measured detector dominates the groups it responds
to, a sloppy one barely pulls. At a fixed point every `N_i/c_i` is 1, so the
weights are irrelevant there — a SAND-II fixed point is a GRAVEL fixed point
and vice versa. Two pinned divergences from SAND-II: zero measurements carry
zero weight and are simply not fitted (they pin nothing to zero — an all-zero
measurement set returns the guess unchanged after one confirming no-op), and
sigmas are required input (zero, non-finite, or missing sigmas raise a clear
error, never silent uniform weighting).

### M1–M6 — MAXED maximum entropy

Where SAND-II, STAYSL-class, and GRAVEL spread each detector's correction
across groups or fit the rates directly, MAXED maximizes the Shannon
relative entropy against the guess-as-prior subject to the chi-square fit —
among the spectra that reproduce the rates it keeps the one closest to the
guess in the information sense:

$$
c_i = \sum_j R_{ij}\,\phi_j
\tag{M1}
$$

$$
S(\phi) = \sum_j \left(\phi_j - \phi^0_j
  - \phi_j \ln(\phi_j / \phi^0_j)\right)
\tag{M2}
$$

$$
\chi^2(\phi) = \sum_i \left(\frac{c_i - N_i}{\sigma_i}\right)^2
\tag{M3}
$$

(M2) is non-positive with equality only at the guess `φ⁰`. Its Lagrange
stationary form under rate constraints is the exponential family in the
per-detector multipliers `λ`:

$$
\phi_j(\lambda) = \phi^0_j \cdot
  \exp\!\left(-\sum_i R_{ij}\,\lambda_i\right)
\tag{M4}
$$

Each cycle linearizes the fold about the current multipliers
(`∂c_i/∂λ_k = −J_ik`) and takes the dual step — one weighted
least-squares solve through the shared `linalg` kernel over the fitted
detectors only:

$$
J_{ik} = \sum_j R_{ij}\,\phi_j\,R_{kj}
\tag{M5}
$$

$$
\min_\delta \sum_{i} \frac{\left(\sum_k H_{ik}\,\delta_k - v_i\right)^2}{{\sigma'}_i^2}
  + \mu\,\|\Delta\|^2,
\qquad \Delta_k = \delta_k / q_k
\tag{M6}
$$

(M6) equilibrates the solve (max-abs row/column scales, with the row scales
compensated into the weights so the caller `1/σ_i²` weights and the fixed
points survive bit-for-bit) plus an adaptive Levenberg-Marquardt damping
term on the physical multiplier step, and accepts only a strictly better
fit that moves no group by more than a factor of `e`. For consistent rates
the multipliers converge to the maximum-entropy exact-fit spectrum; for
inconsistent (redundant disagreeing) readings they converge to the
minimum-chi-square member. The full derivation lives in the
`nucleide-unfold` crate rustdoc (`maxed` module). Like GRAVEL, zero
measurements carry zero weight and are simply not fitted; unlike the other
three methods the run also takes a caller chi-square target (default: the
detector count) — converging in relative change to a fit whose chi-square
still exceeds the target is a hard error, never a partial spectrum.

### The IRDFF-II runtime response pack

Response rows need not be hand-built: `nucleide.data.fetch_irdff` downloads
the official IAEA `IRDFF-II_g725.zip` distribution at runtime and checks its
SHA-256 hash (a mismatch or offline run raises a clear error naming the URL
or both digests), and `parse_irdff_g725` parses the `IRDFF-II.g725` member
into caller-ready rows over the SAND-II 725-group structure for the v1
foil-activation subset (Au-197 and In-115 capture, U-235 and U-238 fission,
Fe-56 and Ni-58 (n,p), Al-27 (n,α), Na-23 (n,2n)) plus the v2 extension of
26 further dosimetry sections (threshold (n,p)/(n,a)/(n,2n) monitors,
capture foils, fission chambers, high-threshold bismuth monitors).
Nothing IAEA-copyright is
vendored — the zip is fetched, never committed — and the rows feed
`sandii`, `staysl`, `gravel`, and `maxed` unchanged. The pointwise isomer reactions
stay out of the pack.

### Underdetermined systems

With fewer detectors than energy groups the system has infinitely many
solutions. Groups no detector responds to (zero-response columns) keep the
guess exactly, but otherwise the adjustment is nonlinear — its weights depend
on the current spectrum — so a general unseen direction is not preserved the
way a linear least-squares null space would be. In the U4 check (6 detectors,
24 groups) the rates are reproduced to `1e-6` and the total guess error is cut by more than half
(measured at about 2.5x): the recoverable bias converges while the unseen
bias keeps the guess.

### Convergence and diagnostics

After each adjustment the largest per-group relative change
`max_j |φ_new − φ_old| / φ_old` is compared against the tolerance; the run
converges when it drops strictly below it. The defaults are `1e-3` and `200`
iterations — library defaults, not values from the report. MAXED
additionally compares the chi-square against its target (default: the
detector count, the chi-square expectation): the run converges only when the
change is below tolerance *and* the fit sits at or below the target. Each adjustment
also reports the refolded rates and the per-detector rate factors
`N_i / c_i`: all ones at a fixed point, and `0.0` for a *pinned* detector (a
zero measurement folding to zero — the `0/0` factor spelled as `0.0`, never
`NaN`).

## Algorithm

Construction validates the whole input set once, naming the offending input
on every rejection: the response matrix must be non-empty, rectangular,
finite, and non-negative; rates finite and non-negative, one per detector;
the guess strictly positive, one value per group; the tolerance finite and
positive; the iteration cap at least 1.

Each iterator step folds the working spectrum (S1), applies the S2/S3
adjustment group by group, and yields the post-adjustment spectrum, refolded
rates, rate factors, and the largest relative change. The weighting has
named, deliberate zero-handling:

- A detector folding to zero contributes no weight (it is skipped).
- A zero response entry `R_ij = 0` skips detector `i` for group `j` — this
  also avoids the `0 · ln 0 = NaN` hazard.
- With no active weight a group keeps the guess (factor 1, exactly).

Zero measurements pin their groups to zero (their adjustment factor is 0). A
positive rate against a zero response row is rejected up front; if pinning
zeroes out the entire support of another detector with a nonzero rate, the
run stops mid-iteration with the same clear error naming that detector. The
`unfold` driver iterates to convergence and returns the solution with its
diagnostics — a halted or non-converged run raises a clear error, never a
silent partial spectrum.

## Worked example: one detector, three groups

A single detector responds to only one of three energy groups:

```python
import nucleide.unfold as unfold

response = [[1.0, 0.0, 0.0]]  # one detector; it responds only to group 0
rates = [4.0]  # its measured rate
guess = [2.0, 7.0, 3.0]  # strictly positive starting spectrum

out = unfold.sandii(response, rates, guess)
out["spectrum"]  # [4.0, 7.0, 3.0]
out["iterations"]  # 2
unfold.forward_fold(response, out["spectrum"])  # [4.0]
```

The first adjustment, by hand: the fold is `c = 1·2 + 0·7 + 0·3 = 2`. Group 0
carries weight `W = R·φ/c = 1·2/2 = 1` from detector 0 only, so its
correction factor is `N/c = 4/2 = 2` and its new value is
`2 · exp(1 · ln 2 / 1) = 4`. Groups 1 and 2 receive no weight, so their
factor is exactly 1 and they keep the guess values `7.0` and `3.0`
bit-for-bit. The largest relative change is `|4 − 2| / 2 = 1.0`, not below
the default `1e-3` tolerance, so a second adjustment runs: the fold is now 4,
the factor is `exp(ln(4/4)) = 1`, the change is `0.0`, and the run converges
with `spectrum = [4.0, 7.0, 3.0]` and `rate_factors = [1.0]`.

This is the underdetermined behavior in miniature: one equation, three
unknowns. The seen group moves to its measured value; the unseen groups keep
the guess — SAND-II cannot and does not invent information no detector
recorded.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **Caller-supplied data, plus one runtime pack**. Response matrix,
  measured rates, guess, and energy-group bounds are all inputs. Evaluated
  libraries (IRDFF and other IAEA-copyright data) are never vendored, and
  the IRDFF-II tabulated group spectra are never used — the benchmark
  shapes below are re-derived from published closed forms with
  citation<Citation id="trkov-2020" />. The one exception to hand-built
  responses is the IRDFF-II runtime pack above: fetched and hash-pinned
  at runtime, never committed.
- **Strictly positive guess required**; response entries and rates must be
  non-negative and finite. Every malformed input raises a clear error naming
  the cause.
- **Detector-uncertainty weighting is the caller's choice of method**:
  SAND-II applies no per-detector statistics factor (see S3); STAYSL-class,
  GRAVEL, and MAXED consume caller-supplied sigmas as weights (see T2/G2/M6:
  `1/σ²`, `N²/σ²`, and `1/σ²` respectively).
- **Underdetermination is real**. With fewer detectors than groups the result
  is not unique: the unseen component of the guess survives unchanged, so
  the unfolded spectrum depends on the guess there. Iteration reduces the
  error; it does not remove it.
- **Zero measurements pin their groups to zero under SAND-II**, and a
  positive rate whose support is pinned away fails with a clear error, up
  front or mid-iteration. GRAVEL and MAXED instead skip zero measurements
  (zero weight, never fitted).
- **Non-convergence is a hard error**: exhausting the iteration cap raises
  (a `ValueError` from Python), never returning a partial spectrum — and for
  MAXED, so does converging in relative change to a fit whose chi-square
  still exceeds the target.
- **No outlier-foil rejection** (the original code's σ-discard feature) and
  no cover-material corrections — recorded as out of scope.
- **Four methods, one at a time**: SAND-II, STAYSL-class, GRAVEL, and MAXED
  share the iterator and diagnostics shape but run separately — there is no
  hybrid that mixes their updates within one run. MAXED closes the recorded
  one-method-per-cycle order; no fifth method is scoped.

## Validation

The crate and `validation/unfold_vs_analytic.py` replay twenty-three pinned
checks, U1–U23, all on synthetic forward-fold-then-recover round-trips — the
response matrices are hand-built Gaussian bumps over log-spaced energy
groups, never evaluated data:

- **U1**: the forward fold matches a hand-written matrix-vector product to
  `1e-12`.
- **U2**: an exact guess is a fixed point — one confirming no-op adjustment
  with a change of exactly `0.0`.
- **U3**: a determined 6-detector, 6-group system is recovered from a
  uniformly 3x-biased guess to `1e-6`.
- **U4**: the underdetermined case — 6 detectors, 24 groups. Rates
  reproduced to `1e-6`; spectrum error cut by more than half, with the
  recoverable bias converging and the unseen bias keeping the guess.
- **U5**: the IRDFF-II analytical benchmark-field shapes named in
  Trkov et al.<Citation id="trkov-2020" /> — a thermal Maxwellian at 293.6 K,
  `φ(E) ∝ E·e^(−E/kT)` with `kT = 8.617333262·10⁻⁵ eV/K × 293.6 K ≈ 0.0253 eV`;
  a pure `1/E` field, `φ(E) ∝ 1/E`; and a Maxwellian at 25 keV,
  `φ(E) ∝ √E·e^(−E/kT)` with `kT = 25 keV` — re-derived from the published
  closed forms and recovered to `1e-5` in a determined probe. The
  IAEA-copyright tabulated spectra are deliberately not used.
- **U6**: exhausting the iteration cap raises a clear error.
- **U7**: unconstrained groups keep the guess bit-for-bit.
- **U8**: malformed inputs raise clear errors naming the cause.
- **U9–U13 (STAYSL-class)**: caller sigmas weight the detectors (a redundant
  reading with 100x the sigma loses to the precise one); a determined 6x6
  system is recovered from a 3x-biased guess to `1e-6`; the underdetermined
  case reproduces rates; the IRDFF-II shapes recover to `1e-5`; a SAND-II
  fixed point is a least-squares fixed point; and the named-contract errors
  (non-convergence, sigma shape, zero/weak responses) raise in four probes.
- **U14–U18 (GRAVEL)**: the same shape — sigma weighting with precise-wins,
  determined recovery to `1e-6`, underdetermined rate reproduction to `1e-6`
  with spectrum error reduced under counting-statistics sigmas, IRDFF-II
  shape recovery to `1e-5`, the cross-method fixed-point check in both
  directions (at a fixed point every rate ratio is 1, so the weights are
  irrelevant), and five named-contract error probes.
- **U19–U23 (MAXED)**: the same shape — `1/σ²` chi-square weighting with
  precise-wins, determined recovery to `1e-6`, underdetermined rate
  reproduction to `1e-6` with spectrum error reduced under
  counting-statistics sigmas, IRDFF-II shape recovery to `1e-5` with group
  bounds bracketing each shape's support (the solve-based house rule), the
  cross-method fixed-point check in both directions (at a fixed point the
  dual residual is zero), and seven named-contract error probes — including
  the chi-square-target hard fail on disagreeing redundant readings.

No external reference implementation exists — PyNE and OpenMC ship no
SAND-II/STAYSL/GRAVEL/MAXED counterpart — so these analytic checks are the
whole correctness story: they always run, and none can be skipped.

## Related work beyond Nucleide

No fifth unfolding method is scoped after MAXED, which closes the recorded
one-method-per-cycle order. Because no external unfolding counterpart exists
in PyNE or OpenMC, cross-code comparison is not available for this
capability.
