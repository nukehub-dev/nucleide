Nucleide implements neutron spectrum unfolding from activation-type
measurements: a caller-supplied guess spectrum is adjusted until folding it
through a caller-supplied response matrix reproduces the measured
detector/reaction rates. The landed method is SAND-II (Spectrum Analysis by
Neutron Detectors II; McElroy et al., AFWL-TR-67-41, 1967)<Citation id="mcelroy-1967" />,
written clean-room from the public-domain report — the adjustment form
reproduced across the open unfolding literature. Every response value,
measured rate, guess entry, and energy-group bound is caller-supplied data:
evaluated libraries such as IRDFF are IAEA-copyright and are never vendored.
The IRDFF-II analytical benchmark-field shapes used in the correctness checks
below are re-derived from their published closed forms<Citation id="trkov-2020" /> —
the library's tabulated group spectra are never read. The S1–S3 labels are
the crate's pinned equation labels; U1–U8 label its correctness checks.

## Overview

Foil-activation spectrometry — the JET/ITER diagnostics workhorse — measures a
handful of detector/reaction rates and asks for the neutron spectrum behind
them, one value per energy group. The forward problem is a matrix
multiplication (the *fold*); the inverse problem has no unique answer whenever
there are fewer detectors than energy groups, which is the normal case.
Unfolding therefore does not invent the unseen part: it iteratively adjusts a
caller-supplied guess until the fold reproduces the measurements, moving only
the components the detectors can see. It is inverse-problem iteration, not a
transport solve.

The Python surface `nucleide.unfold` (a thin facade over the crate) exposes
two entry points: `sandii(...)`, which runs the adjustment and returns the
converged spectrum with diagnostics, and `forward_fold(...)`, the shared
forward operator.

<Callout type="info" title="Code shape">
  The crate mirrors the other solver crates: `sandii.rs` holds the SAND-II
  adjustment iterator and the `unfold` driver, `lib.rs` holds the shared
  forward operator (`forward_fold`) and the input validation, and `error.rs`
  is a named error set (a `#[non_exhaustive]` enum with a `Result` alias).
</Callout>

## Mathematical details

Definitions: a response matrix `R` with one row per detector/reaction (entry
`R_ij ≥ 0` per energy group `j`), measured rates `N_i ≥ 0`, and a guess
spectrum `φ_j > 0` (strictly positive — the update is multiplicative).

### S1 — Forward fold

$$
c_i = \sum_j R_{ij}\,\phi_j
\tag{S1}
$$

The calculated rate `c_i` of detector `i`. This same map is how the
correctness checks synthesize measured rates from a known spectrum.

### S2 — Detector rate-share weights

$$
W_{ji} = R_{ij}\,\phi_j / c_i
\tag{S2}
$$

The share of detector `i`'s calculated rate contributed by group `j`; the
shares sum to one over `j` for each detector with a nonzero fold.

### S3 — Multiplicative adjustment

$$
\phi_j \leftarrow \phi_j \cdot
\exp\!\left( \frac{\sum_i W_{ji}\,\ln(N_i / c_i)}{\sum_i W_{ji}} \right)
\tag{S3}
$$

One adjustment per iteration: the new spectrum is the old one times a weighted
geometric mean of the per-detector correction factors `N_i / c_i`, weighted
by the S2 rate shares. Three properties follow directly:

- **Positivity**: a strictly positive guess stays non-negative — and stays
  strictly positive when all measured rates are positive (a zero measurement
  pins its groups to exactly zero).
- **Fixed point**: a guess that already reproduces the rates is unchanged
  (every correction factor is 1).
- **Unseen groups keep the guess**: a group no detector responds to has
  `Σ_i W_ji = 0`; its adjustment factor is exactly 1.

`W_ji` is the canonical base SAND-II weight. The original implementation's
optional per-detector statistics factor (`N_i²/σ_i`) is not applied, so
adjustments are not weighted by detector uncertainty<Citation id="mcelroy-1967" />.

### Underdetermined systems

With fewer detectors than energy groups the system has infinitely many
solutions. Groups no detector responds to (zero-response columns) keep the
guess exactly, but otherwise the adjustment is nonlinear — its weights depend
on the current spectrum — so a general unseen direction is not preserved the
way a linear least-squares null space would be. In the U4 check (6 detectors,
24 groups) the rates are reproduced to `1e-6` and the total guess error is cut by more than half
(measured at about 2.5x): the recoverable bias converges while the unseen
bias keeps the guess.

### Convergence and diagnostics

After each adjustment the largest per-group relative change
`max_j |φ_new − φ_old| / φ_old` is compared against the tolerance; the run
converges when it drops strictly below it. The defaults are `1e-3` and `200`
iterations — library defaults, not values from the report. Each adjustment
also reports the refolded rates and the per-detector rate factors
`N_i / c_i`: all ones at a fixed point, and `0.0` for a *pinned* detector (a
zero measurement folding to zero — the `0/0` factor spelled as `0.0`, never
`NaN`).

## Algorithm

Construction validates the whole input set once, naming the offending input
on every rejection: the response matrix must be non-empty, rectangular,
finite, and non-negative; rates finite and non-negative, one per detector;
the guess strictly positive, one value per group; the tolerance finite and
positive; the iteration cap at least 1.

Each iterator step folds the working spectrum (S1), applies the S2/S3
adjustment group by group, and yields the post-adjustment spectrum, refolded
rates, rate factors, and the largest relative change. The weighting has
named, deliberate zero-handling:

- A detector folding to zero contributes no weight (it is skipped).
- A zero response entry `R_ij = 0` skips detector `i` for group `j` — this
  also avoids the `0 · ln 0 = NaN` hazard.
- With no active weight a group keeps the guess (factor 1, exactly).

Zero measurements pin their groups to zero (their adjustment factor is 0). A
positive rate against a zero response row is rejected up front; if pinning
zeroes out the entire support of another detector with a nonzero rate, the
run stops mid-iteration with the same clear error naming that detector. The
`unfold` driver iterates to convergence and returns the solution with its
diagnostics — a halted or non-converged run raises a clear error, never a
silent partial spectrum.

## Worked example: one detector, three groups

A single detector responds to only one of three energy groups:

```python
import nucleide.unfold as unfold

response = [[1.0, 0.0, 0.0]]  # one detector; it responds only to group 0
rates = [4.0]  # its measured rate
guess = [2.0, 7.0, 3.0]  # strictly positive starting spectrum

out = unfold.sandii(response, rates, guess)
out["spectrum"]  # [4.0, 7.0, 3.0]
out["iterations"]  # 2
unfold.forward_fold(response, out["spectrum"])  # [4.0]
```

The first adjustment, by hand: the fold is `c = 1·2 + 0·7 + 0·3 = 2`. Group 0
carries weight `W = R·φ/c = 1·2/2 = 1` from detector 0 only, so its
correction factor is `N/c = 4/2 = 2` and its new value is
`2 · exp(1 · ln 2 / 1) = 4`. Groups 1 and 2 receive no weight, so their
factor is exactly 1 and they keep the guess values `7.0` and `3.0`
bit-for-bit. The largest relative change is `|4 − 2| / 2 = 1.0`, not below
the default `1e-3` tolerance, so a second adjustment runs: the fold is now 4,
the factor is `exp(ln(4/4)) = 1`, the change is `0.0`, and the run converges
with `spectrum = [4.0, 7.0, 3.0]` and `rate_factors = [1.0]`.

This is the underdetermined behavior in miniature: one equation, three
unknowns. The seen group moves to its measured value; the unseen groups keep
the guess — SAND-II cannot and does not invent information no detector
recorded.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **Caller-supplied data only**. Response matrix, measured rates, guess, and
  energy-group bounds are all inputs. Evaluated libraries (IRDFF and other
  IAEA-copyright data) are never vendored, and the IRDFF-II tabulated group
  spectra are never used — the benchmark shapes below are re-derived from
  published closed forms with citation<Citation id="trkov-2020" />. A
  runtime-download data pack stays a later decision.
- **Strictly positive guess required**; response entries and rates must be
  non-negative and finite. Every malformed input raises a clear error naming
  the cause.
- **No detector-uncertainty weighting**: the original code's optional
  per-detector statistics factor is not applied (see S3).
- **Underdetermination is real**. With fewer detectors than groups the result
  is not unique: the unseen component of the guess survives unchanged, so
  the unfolded spectrum depends on the guess there. Iteration reduces the
  error; it does not remove it.
- **Zero measurements pin their groups to zero**, and a positive rate whose
  support is pinned away fails with a clear error, up front or
  mid-iteration.
- **Non-convergence is a hard error**: exhausting the iteration cap raises
  (a `ValueError` from Python), never returning a partial spectrum.
- **No outlier-foil rejection** (the original code's σ-discard feature) and
  no cover-material corrections — recorded as out of scope.
- **SAND-II only**, one method per cycle, behind the same iterator shape.

## Validation

The crate and `validation/unfold_vs_analytic.py` replay eight pinned checks,
U1–U8, all on synthetic forward-fold-then-recover round-trips — the response
matrices are hand-built Gaussian bumps over log-spaced energy groups, never
evaluated data:

- **U1**: the forward fold matches a hand-written matrix-vector product to
  `1e-12`.
- **U2**: an exact guess is a fixed point — one confirming no-op adjustment
  with a change of exactly `0.0`.
- **U3**: a determined 6-detector, 6-group system is recovered from a
  uniformly 3x-biased guess to `1e-6`.
- **U4**: the underdetermined case — 6 detectors, 24 groups. Rates
  reproduced to `1e-6`; spectrum error cut by more than half, with the
  recoverable bias converging and the unseen bias keeping the guess.
- **U5**: the IRDFF-II analytical benchmark-field shapes named in
  Trkov et al.<Citation id="trkov-2020" /> — a thermal Maxwellian at 293.6 K,
  `φ(E) ∝ E·e^(−E/kT)` with `kT = 8.617333262·10⁻⁵ eV/K × 293.6 K ≈ 0.0253 eV`;
  a pure `1/E` field, `φ(E) ∝ 1/E`; and a Maxwellian at 25 keV,
  `φ(E) ∝ √E·e^(−E/kT)` with `kT = 25 keV` — re-derived from the published
  closed forms and recovered to `1e-5` in a determined probe. The
  IAEA-copyright tabulated spectra are deliberately not used.
- **U6**: exhausting the iteration cap raises a clear error.
- **U7**: unconstrained groups keep the guess bit-for-bit.
- **U8**: malformed inputs raise clear errors naming the cause.

No external reference implementation exists — PyNE and OpenMC ship no
SAND-II counterpart — so these analytic checks are the whole correctness
story: they always run, and none can be skipped.

## Related work beyond Nucleide

Adjacent methods recorded for later cycles, each to land in its own module
behind the same iterator and diagnostics shape: STAYSL-class least-squares
adjustment (Perey, ORNL/TM-6062, 1977) on the shared `nucleide-linalg`
least-squares kernel; GRAVEL (Matzke, PTB-N-19, 1994), the SAND-II update
with measurement-error weights; and MAXED (Reginatto & Goldhagen, Health
Phys. 77 (1999) 579) maximum entropy. A vendored response library (a
runtime-download pack) also stays a later decision. Because no external
SAND-II counterpart exists in PyNE or OpenMC, cross-code comparison is not
available for this capability.
