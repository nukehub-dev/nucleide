Nucleide solves nuclide transmutation over a single time step using a **burnup
matrix** formulation and the **Chebyshev Rational Approximation Method (CRAM)**
for the matrix exponential.

## Overview

For a vector of nuclide atom densities $\mathbf{n}(t)$, the linearized depletion
equation is

$$
\frac{d\mathbf{n}}{dt} = \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{n},
\qquad
\mathbf{n}(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\,\mathbf{n}(0),
$$

where $\mathbf{A}$ is the **burnup matrix**. Each column of $\mathbf{A}$
describes how a parent nuclide is lost and what it produces; each row describes
how a daughter nuclide is gained.

<Callout type="info" title="Where this lives in the code">
  Matrix construction is in `crates/depletion/src/matrix.rs` and the CRAM solver
  is in `crates/depletion/src/cram.rs`. The public driver is `deplete` in
  `crates/depletion/src/lib.rs`.
</Callout>

## Building the burnup matrix

Nucleide builds $\mathbf{A}$ from a [`Chain`](nuclear-data.mdx) and a set of
one-group reaction rates.

### Decay

For nuclide $i$ with half-life $t_{1/2,i}$, the decay constant is

$$
\lambda_i = \frac{\ln 2}{t_{1/2,i}}.
$$

The diagonal entry loses atoms at rate $\lambda_i$:

$$
A_{ii} \;\mathrel{-}=\; \lambda_i.
$$

Each decay mode with branching ratio $b_{i \to j}$ adds production to the
off-diagonal:

$$
A_{ji} \;\mathrel{+}=\; \lambda_i \, b_{i \to j}.
$$

### Reactions

For a one-group reaction rate $\sigma\phi$ of type $r$ on nuclide $i$:

- **Loss**: $A_{ii} \mathrel{-}= \sigma\phi$ once per reaction *type*, even if
  the chain lists multiple branched entries for that type.
- **Production**: each reaction entry with branching ratio $b$ and target $j$
  contributes
  $$
  A_{ji} \;\mathrel{+}=\; \sigma\phi \, b.
  $$
- **Light-particle secondaries**: reactions such as $(n,\alpha)$, $(n,p)$, and
  $(n,d)$ emit light nuclides that are tracked when they appear in the chain
  (e.g. $(n,\alpha) \to \mathrm{He\text{-}4}$).
- **Fission**: if fission yields $y_{i \to k}$ are present, production is
  distributed over all fission products:
  $$
  A_{ki} \;\mathrel{+}=\; \sigma\phi \, y_{i \to k}.
  $$

The final matrix $\mathbf{A}$ has units of $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and is sparse, with
one diagonal entry per chain nuclide even if it is stable.

## CRAM: matrix exponential by partial fractions

CRAM evaluates the action of the matrix exponential $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$ on
$\mathbf{n}_0$ in **incomplete partial fraction (IPF)** product form:

$$
\mathbf{n}_k \;=\; \mathbf{n}_{k-1} \;+\;
2 \, \mathrm{Re}\!\left[
\alpha_k \, (\mathbf{A}t - \theta_k \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{n}_{k-1}
\right],
\qquad
\mathbf{n}(t) = \alpha_0 \, \mathbf{n}_m,
$$

where $m = 8$ for order-16 and $m = 24$ for order-48. The coefficients
$\alpha_0$, $\alpha_k$, and $\theta_k$ are pre-computed complex numbers
published by Pusa. Each pole is applied sequentially to the running solution
vector; these IPF coefficients are not interchangeable with the residues of
the classic partial-fraction sum form.

<Callout type="tip" title="Why the IPF product form?">
  Each step $(\mathbf{A}t - \theta_k \mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{n}_{k-1}$ is a
  sparse linear solve rather than a full matrix exponential. Nucleide uses a
  sparse LU factorization from the `linalg` facade, and the symbolic pattern is
  reused across all poles.
</Callout>

cheaper; CRAM-48 is the production default.

## Analytic Bateman fast path

For decay-only chains the burnup matrix is lower-triangular $\mathbf{\Lambda}$
and the depletion equation admits a closed form (Bateman, *Proc. Cambridge
Philos. Soc.* 15 (1910), 423–427)<Citation id="bateman-1910" />:

$$
\frac{d\mathbf{N}}{dt} = \mathbf{\Lambda}\,\mathbf{N},
\qquad
\Lambda_{jj} = -\lambda_j,
\qquad
\Lambda_{ij} = b_{j \to i}\,\lambda_j \;\; (i > j),
\qquad
\lambda_j = \frac{\ln 2}{t_{1/2,j}},
\tag{E1}
$$

with $\lambda_j = 0$ for stable nuclides. $\mathbf{\Lambda}$ is assembled from
the same decay data as $\mathbf{A}$ (spontaneous-fission daughters skipped,
$\mathrm{He\text{-}4}$/$\mathrm{H\text{-}1}$ secondaries from alpha/proton
decay included).

When the diagonal entries are distinct and the chain is acyclic, the
eigendecomposition $\mathbf{\Lambda} = \mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{C}^{-1}$
with $\mathbf{D} = \mathrm{diag}(-\lambda)$ has the unit-diagonal recursion
(Amaku–Pascholati–Vanin)<Citation id="amaku-2010" />

$$
C_{ii} = 1,
\qquad
C_{ij} = \sum_{k=j}^{i-1} \frac{\Lambda_{ik}\,C_{kj}}{\Lambda_{jj} - \Lambda_{ii}}
\;\; (i > j),
\tag{E2,E3}
$$

$$
C^{-1}_{ii} = 1,
\qquad
C^{-1}_{ij} = -\sum_{k=j}^{i-1} C_{ik}\,C^{-1}_{kj}
\;\; (i > j),
\tag{E4}
$$

$$
\mathbf{N}(t) = \mathbf{C}\,\mathrm{diag}(e^{-\lambda t})\,\mathbf{C}^{-1}\,\mathbf{N}_0.
\tag{E5}
$$

Activity and cumulative-decay observables follow (E8) of
`crates/depletion/src/bateman.rs`: $A_i = \lambda_i N_i$ (implemented by
`activity_vec` in `crates/depletion/src/integrate.rs`) with the diagonal
cumulative integral $\int_0^t e^{-\lambda s}\,ds = (1 - e^{-\lambda t})/\lambda$
($\lambda = 0$ limit $\to t$; `cumulative_decays` in
`crates/depletion/src/inventory.rs`). Labels (E6)–(E7) are intentionally
unassigned in both the code and this page so the two stay consistent; a
code-side close-the-gap renumber is left to the depletion owner (no formula
changes either way).

$\mathbf{C}$/$\mathbf{C}^{-1}$ are time-independent, so Nucleide builds them
once per chain (`BatemanCache` in `crates/depletion/src/bateman.rs`,
generated at runtime from the crate's own chain data) and reuses them across
timesteps; each solve is two triangular matvecs. The single-chain Bateman sum
is the special case of this form for a linear chain, and the two/three-nuclide
$A \to B \to C$ formulas in the worked example above drop out directly.

Two precision variants share the cache: `bateman` (plain accumulation) and
`bateman_hp` (f64-careful summation only — per-row terms sorted by magnitude
and summed with Neumaier compensation, plus `exp_m1` forms for small
$\lambda t$; no extended-precision types, no new dependencies). On a
synthetic 10-order-spread chain (year-scale parent, microsecond-scale
progeny) the HP variant beats-or-matches standard summation while both
conserve atoms to $10^{-8}$.

<Callout type="note" title="Fast-path limits (D1–D4)">
  The dispatcher falls back to CRAM-48 — never an error — when the closed
  form does not apply: (D1) near-equal decay constants with a live coupling
  path ($|\lambda_j - \lambda_i| \le 10^{-12}\max$, uncoupled pairs such as
  two stables take the limit form $C_{ij} = 0$ instead); (D2) cyclic or
  out-of-order decay topology (any gain above the diagonal); (D3) stable
  nuclides ($\lambda = 0$, handled inline via $e^0 = 1$ limit forms);
  (D4) reactions or fission active (assembled matrix differs from the
  pure-decay reconstruction). A zero timestep returns the input exactly.
  Select the kernel with `method="cram16"`, `"cram48"`, `"bateman"`, or
  `"bateman_hp"` on `deplete`, `deplete_series`, `DepletionSystem.solve`,
  and `Inventory.decay` (default `"cram48"`).
</Callout>

## Worked example: sequential decay chain

Consider a three-nuclide chain:

$$
A \xrightarrow{\lambda_A} B \xrightarrow{\lambda_B} C,
$$

with $\lambda_A = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$,
$\lambda_B = 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, and $C$ stable. The burnup matrix is

$$
\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix}
-\lambda_A & 0 & 0 \\
\lambda_A & -\lambda_B & 0 \\
0 & \lambda_B & 0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Starting from $n_A(0) = 10^{15}$ atoms and $n_B(0) = n_C(0) = 0$, the analytic
solution after $t = 10^5\,\mathrm{s}$ is

<Steps>
  <Step>
    $n_A(t) = n_A(0)\,e^{-\lambda_A t} = 10^{15}\,e^{-0.1} \approx 9.0484 \times 10^{14}$.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    $n_B(t) = n_A(0)\,\frac{\lambda_A}{\lambda_B - \lambda_A}\,
    (e^{-\lambda_A t} - e^{-\lambda_B t}) \approx 5.9662 \times 10^{13}$.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    $n_C(t) = n_A(0) - n_A(t) - n_B(t) \approx 3.5501 \times 10^{13}$.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Nucleide's CRAM-48 result matches this analytic solution to better than one part
in $10^7$ for $n_B$ and one part in $10^9$ for $n_C$. This exact chain is
exercised in `crates/depletion/src/lib.rs` as `analytic_two_step_decay_cram48`.

## Time-series integrators

For schedules with per-step rates, `crates/depletion/src/integrate.rs`
chains single-step CRAM solves. Each `Step` carries a length `dt` in seconds
plus prescribed one-group rates in the same layout as
`DepletionSystem::build`; the matrix is rebuilt from each step's rates, so
time-varying rates are handled exactly per step.
`Integrator` selects the method: `Predictor` (one CRAM solve per step),
`Cecm` (predictor plus a corrector re-solve from the step start with the
averaged matrix), or `Cf4` (fourth-order commutator-free quasi-Magnus, two
half-step CRAM solves). Each step's exponential additionally honors the
`Method` kernel: `Cram` runs the CRAM order named above, while
`Bateman`/`BatemanHp` evaluate the analytic closed form per step (steps with
live rates fall back to CRAM-48; CF4 stages scale the timestep rather than
the matrix so per-stage systems stay in decay form). Because rates here are
prescribed rather than recomputed from the predicted composition, the
corrector matrix equals the predictor matrix and `Cecm` reproduces
`Predictor` up to roundoff; the two-solve structure is kept so flux-coupled
extensions can plug a recomputed matrix into the same code path. Under the
same piecewise-constant reconstruction each `Cf4` stage is a half-step
exponential, so its accuracy is at least as good as `Predictor` on the
analytic test chain.

Results come back as `TimeSeries` with one row per time node in
chain-nuclide order (`times[0] == 0.0` echoes the initial state):

- `times`: cumulative time nodes in seconds.
- `atoms`: atom counts per node.
- `activity`: $A_i = \lambda_i N_i$ in Bq, clamped at zero.
- `decay_heat`: $H_i = \lambda_i N_i E_i$ in W, clamped at zero.

Per-nuclide decay energies resolve chain-first: the chain's `decay_energy`
attribute (eV converted to MeV) when positive and finite, else the
ENDF/B-VII.1 prompt-decay-energy table
(`nucleide_nuclei::data::decay_energy_mev_by_name`), else `0.0` (stable and
synthetic nuclides) — one evaluated table, no parallel hand-kept fallback.

<Callout type="note" title="Python exposure">
  `nucleide.depletion.deplete_series` wraps `integrate` with an `integrator`
  argument (`"predictor"`, `"cecm"`, `"cf4"`) and a `method` argument
  (`"cram16"`, `"cram48"`, `"bateman"`, `"bateman_hp"`; an explicitly
  non-default `method` overrides `order`). The per-step `decay_heat`
  maps use the same chain → table → `0.0` resolution as the core, so they
  match `integrate` exactly; the core `t = 0` row is omitted. See the
  [Run depletion tutorial](../tutorials/python/run-depletion.md).
</Callout>

## Year-length convention

Production code uses the **Julian year**: 1 yr = 365.25 d = 31,557,600 s
(`SECONDS_PER_YEAR` in `crates/depletion/src/inventory.rs`; the `y` unit in
`crates/alara-io/src/schedule.rs` and `crates/alara-io/src/deck.rs`).
Rationale: the Julian year is the standard year-scale interval for
time-unit conversion, and both the depletion solver and the ALARA
schedule/deck layers convert through it.

Two deliberate neighbors differ and must not be “unified” by accident:

- `crates/fispact-io/src/inventory.rs` uses a 365-day year to match the
  ALARA reference tool’s file-format convention — format fidelity, not physics.
- Some test anchors divide by the Gregorian mean year 365.2425 d
  ($3.1556952 \times 10^7$ s/yr), e.g. the Cs-137 30.08 yr anchor in the
  `nuclei` half-life tests. The gap between conventions there is
  $\approx 6 \times 10^{-4}$ yr against a $0.01$ yr tolerance, so no
  validation gate moves either way.

Decision (no gate movement): keep the Julian production convention, document
the split here, and migrate test anchors to 31,557,600 s/yr only where the
current tolerance already hides the drift — tolerances themselves stay
untouched. The `alara-io` and `fispact-io` year definitions are referenced
from here rather than restated.

## Assumptions and limitations

- **One-group reaction rates**: CRAM in Nucleide uses a single scalar rate per
  reaction channel. Multigroup fluxes must be collapsed externally.
- **Fixed cross sections over the step**: $\mathbf{A}$ is treated as constant
  during $t$; multi-step schedules rebuild it from each step's prescribed
  rates, with no flux renormalization between steps.
- **Fission yields are single-energy**: the yield set at the lowest incident
  neutron energy is used, matching OpenMC's default.
- **Decay branching ratios**: chains read from XML keep the file values
  verbatim; only programmatically built chains are renormalized (the largest
  branch is adjusted so the modes sum to exactly 1.0).
- **Stable nuclides have zero diagonal**: they act as sinks in the chain.
  The Bateman fast path handles them through limit forms ($e^0 = 1$).
- **CRAM order**: order-16 is adequate for short, simple chains; order-48 is
  recommended for production burnup matrices. Pure-decay chains may use the
  `bateman`/`bateman_hp` closed form instead (same `method=` selector;
  automatic CRAM-48 fallback outside decay-only triangular topology).
- **Bateman conditioning**: the closed form sums alternating-sign terms, so
  long chains (tens of members) or extreme half-life spreads can suffer
  catastrophic cancellation in float64 — `bateman_hp` (sorted Neumaier
  summation) extends the usable range, but ill-conditioned chains should use
  CRAM-48, which remains the default. The automatic fallback covers
  non-triangular topology, not ill-conditioning: pick the method deliberately
  for long chains.

## Related work beyond Nucleide

Adjacent capabilities found in PyNE, OpenMC, and the literature that Nucleide
does not implement:

- **Multi-step time integration**: OpenMC ships predictor-corrector
  integrators (CECM, CELI, CF4, EPCRK4, and their SI variants) that recompute
  reaction rates between steps; Nucleide's `Integrator::{Predictor, Cecm,
  Cf4}` uses prescribed per-step rates, so `Cecm` currently degenerates to
  `Predictor`.
- **Substepping**: OpenMC's CRAM solver can subdivide a step while reusing the
  LU factorizations; Nucleide has no substep parameter.
- **Transport-coupled depletion**: OpenMC normalizes tally reaction rates to
  absolute rates from a power or source normalization; Nucleide assumes rates
  are already given in $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.
- **Energy-dependent fission yields**: OpenMC selects between constant,
  thermal, and thermal+fast yield modes; Nucleide uses the lowest-energy
  yield set.
- **Feed and removal terms**: OpenMC adds transfer rates and external source
  vectors to the burnup matrix for MSR-style continuous processing.
- **Chain truncation**: PyNE's transmutagen-generated solver truncates the
  transmutation tree by density tolerance; Nucleide uses a fixed chain.
- **Krylov subspace methods**: an alternative to CRAM for the Bateman
  equations; see Josey et al. (2017)<Citation id="josey-2017" />.
- **Precomputed decay datasets**: the `radioactivedecay` package ships
  precomputed decay matrices for fast lookup; Nucleide's `BatemanCache`
  follows the same time-independent-cache idea but generates the
  eigendecomposition at runtime from its own chain data (clean-room
  textbook implementation, no vendored data).
