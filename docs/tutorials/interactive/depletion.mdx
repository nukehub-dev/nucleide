Depletion solves the Bateman equation for atom densities $\vec{n}(t)$:

$$
\frac{d\vec{n}}{dt} = A \vec{n},
\qquad
\vec{n}(t) = e^{A t} \vec{n}_0
$$

where $A$ is the transmutation matrix built from decay, capture, and fission
reaction rates. Nucleide uses the Chebyshev Rational Approximation Method
(CRAM) with published 16- and 48-order coefficients.

## Depletion chain

A chain XML lists nuclides, half-lives, decay branches, reaction targets, and
fission yields. The demo below uses a tiny three-nuclide chain:

$$
\text{I-135} \xrightarrow{\beta} \text{Xe-135} \xrightarrow{\beta} \text{Cs-135}
$$

## One-step solve

`deplete(chain, n0, dt, rates, order)` integrates over one time step `dt`. In
production you would loop this call, update reaction rates from a transport
solve, and accumulate burnup. The demo also offers a **Burnup curve** run that
repeats the step and plots nuclide densities versus cumulative time.
