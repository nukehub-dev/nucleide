Material leaving a regulated radiation area can be cleared for reuse or
disposal when its activity concentrations are low enough. **EU Council
Directive 2013/59/Euratom, Annex VII Table A** tabulates default activity
concentrations (in Bq/g) for solid material, and the screening criterion is
the **sum-of-fractions rule**: add up each nuclide's activity divided by its
table level, and the material passes when the sum is at or below one.

The demo below screens an editable nuclide inventory against the default EU
table. It reports the per-nuclide fractions, the sum, the dominant
contributor, and whether the screening criterion is satisfied. This is
screening arithmetic, not a compliance decision — real clearance requires
the governing regulatory table, material bookkeeping, and the national
transposition of the directive.

## Radiological totals (S3–S7)

The same activation inventories feed five more pure-arithmetic totals. The
committed ingestion and inhalation hazards (S4/S5) fold per-nuclide
activities with caller 50-year committed dose coefficients into total doses
in Sv, each with an excluding-tritium companion. The transport ratio (S6)
folds activities with caller A₂ limits into the dimensionless total Bq/A₂
plus the effective A₂ it defines. The IAEA clearance-index variant (S7)
folds activities with caller IAEA levels and the total mass into a
dimensionless index screened at ≤ 1. The slab/point gamma dose (S3) folds a
specific activity with caller group yields and attenuation coefficients into
Sv/h, clamping short point distances loudly at 0.3 m. Every table behind
these kernels — dose coefficients, A₂ limits, IAEA levels, attenuation data —
stays a caller input and is never vendored. The demo below exercises all
five over editable inventories.
